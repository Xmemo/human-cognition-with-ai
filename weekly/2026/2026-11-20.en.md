## Executive judgment

This week's strongest contribution is a **measurement audit**, not evidence of general cognitive decline. A systematic realist synthesis of 93 higher-education studies found that 69 lacked a learning outcome measured independently of AI and 49 did not report how learners handled AI output. A four-week quasi-experiment offers bounded support for design-thinking scaffolds while a separate cross-sectional survey associates LLM dependence with lower self-reported metacognitive awareness and critical thinking. A human–AI timing-game preprint identifies a different group-level boundary: AI agents can approximate probabilistic timing strategies but were less adaptive to partners over repeated interaction. Designs, outcomes and causal strength differ markedly.

## Verified evidence

### 1. Beyond the tool: a systematic realist synthesis of cognitive agency in human-GenAI interaction in higher education

**Chaogang Li, Mohd Fadzil Abdul Hanid & Mengjie Zhang** · *International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education* · **2026-11-18** · **NEW** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R4 R6** · **DOI:** [10.1186/s41239-026-00644-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00644-3) · [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-026-00644-3)

A PRISMA-guided realist synthesis appraised **93 peer-reviewed studies**. **49** did not report learner behavior toward AI output; **69** lacked a learning measure independent of AI assistance; only **four** reported processing evidence together with such a measure. Under strict manifest coding, 21 studies supplied evidence on both cognitive-allocation axes. The proposed configurations describe hypotheses about *what* is delegated and *how* outputs are scrutinized, not a pooled causal effect.

**Caveat:** The corpus covers 2021–2025 higher-education research; heterogeneous methods, missing outcome reporting and model-guided coding prevent causal or universal conclusions.

**Why it matters:** The literature's measurement deficit constrains stronger claims about cognitive agency and retained learning.

### 2. Design-thinking scaffolds for learner-agency-preserving AI use: effects on creative self-efficacy, cognitive flexibility, and engagement

**Yueyong Wang** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-11-13** · **NEW** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R4 R5** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1948254](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1948254) · [Publisher](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1948254/full)

A four-wave quasi-experiment compared non-AI, ordinary GenAI and design-thinking-scaffolded GenAI learning among **162 students in six intact classes** over four weeks. Relative to ordinary GenAI, scaffolded use showed higher adjusted self-reported creative self-efficacy (0.33), cognitive flexibility (0.35) and engagement (0.35), plus higher independently rated team-product quality (20.14 versus 17.62). Coded prompts reflected more critique and comparison.

**Caveat:** Only six nonrandomized class clusters; self-reported psychological outcomes, bundled scaffold components and no delayed AI-free transfer test. No single active mechanism can be identified.

**Why it matters:** A promising bounded intervention, not proof that more friction or any particular scaffold universally improves learning.

### 3. AI usage, large language model dependence, and critical thinking among university students: the mediating roles of metacognitive awareness and AI literacy

**Sha Ouyang, XinLiang Huang, Ruihua Li & Xi Chen** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-11-18** · **NEW** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R6** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1822091](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1822091) · [Publisher PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1822091/pdf)

A cross-sectional survey of **878 Chinese university students** distinguished usage frequency from habitual LLM dependence. Dependence was negatively associated with self-reported metacognitive awareness, AI literacy and critical thinking; structural-equation pathways also linked usage to dependence.

**Caveat:** Self-report, common-method bias and cross-sectional modeling do not identify causal mediation, objectively measured thinking skill or future decline.

**Why it matters:** Frequency of use and dependence should not be treated as interchangeable measures.

### 4. Rational Use of Time for Social Coordination in Humans and AI

**Xiulin Yang, Sirisha Gudavalli & Jennifer Hu** · *arXiv preprint* · **2026-11-10** · **NEW** · **[C]** · **Radars: R5 R7 R8 R9** · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2611.14766](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2611.14766) · [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14766)

A cooperative card-game study evaluated **150 humans** and computer-use / text-prompted AI models in coordination relying on timing cues without explicit messages. Bayesian belief inference described human behavior and some frontier-model behavior; tested AI models did not adapt as efficiently to partners over repeated play.

**Caveat:** Unreviewed preprint, narrow game ecology, selected models and timing interfaces; does not establish general AI social incompetence or persistent cultural change.

**Why it matters:** Partner adaptation over time is distinct from one-shot social inference, an important boundary for hybrid-group research.

## Evidence convergence, counterevidence and uncertainty

The systematic synthesis identifies why output-quality and self-report studies cannot establish AI-independent learning. The scaffolded classroom study provides positive short-run evidence, while the dependence survey supplies only associations: neither warrants a claim that AI use generally harms cognition. The timing-game result is a different, group-level limitation rather than confirmation of an individual deskilling theory. No new R7 cultural-persistence finding qualified for selection.
