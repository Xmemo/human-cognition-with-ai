## Verified evidence

### 1. Human learning is an understudied but promising lever for boosting human–AI synergy

**Julian Berger, Jason W. Burton, Ralph Hertwig, Thomas Kosch, Ralf H. J. M. Kurvers, Benito Kurzenberger, Christopher Lazik, Linda Onnasch, Tobias Rieger, Anna I. Thoma, Dirk U. Wulff & Stefan M. Herzog** · *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* · **2026-09-21** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R5 R8** · DOI: [10.1073/pnas.2536100123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2536100123)

Reanalysis of 74 studies found only ten included outcome feedback. The feedback versus no-feedback synergy contrast was g = 0.34 (95% credible interval −0.01 to 0.69). Neither overall subgroup estimate was credibly different from zero. No included study randomized feedback availability. This is a tentative moderator signal, not proof of causal improvement or independent skill retention.

### 2. Effects of structured generative AI feedback on data science students' critical thinking: a propensity score matching approach

**Han Yin, Nan Tao, Shengbing Wu, Yifu Wang & Tianyue Niu** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-11-11** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R4** · DOI: [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1924393](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1924393/full)

A three-month data science course compared structured AI feedback with conventional instructor feedback in two intact classes (167 students). After propensity matching, the adjusted critical-thinking difference was 5.64 points (95% CI 3.62–7.65). Matching cannot remove class-level or unobserved confounding; the bundled intervention and adapted assessment do not establish delayed unaided transfer.

### 3. From dyad to triad: human–AI collaboration and the changing roles of teachers and learners in formal education

**Matthias Conrad** · *Frontiers in Education* · **2026-11-23** · **NEW** · **[C]** · **Radars: R2 R4 R5 R6** · DOI: [10.3389/feduc.2026.2009337](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.2009337/full)

This theoretical paper describes formal education as a teacher–learner–AI triad and discusses fluency, trust calibration and human epistemic responsibility. It provides a vocabulary for role allocation, not a tested intervention or validated measure.
