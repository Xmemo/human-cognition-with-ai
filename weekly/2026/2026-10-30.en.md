## Executive judgment

The evidence now draws a sharper distinction between **assisted outcomes**, **short-lag transfer**, **calibrated correction**, and **population-level diversity**. A new nursing-education meta-analysis reports positive but highly heterogeneous teaching effects. A broader learning meta-analysis shows why a large pooled mean does not predict success in a new setting. Randomized scaffold research finds that greater cognitive effort and lower perceived helpfulness can accompany better writing and three-day recall, while an older quasi-experiment found no incremental benefit from simply increasing scaffold intensity. A large human–AI oversight preprint reports that tested assistance can reduce human correction of AI errors. Newly discovered creative-population evidence makes mean–variance tradeoffs concrete without proving universal cultural decline.

**Baseline decision: NO_CHANGE.** The 2026-08-29 Control / Retention / Variance model remains a useful research organizer, not a validated universal theory. No new Baseline or long-lived gap-ledger edit is justified.

## Verified evidence

### 1. A meta-analysis of the effects of generative artificial intelligence on learning, cognitive, and linguistic outcomes

**Begzod Kh. Khodjaev, Alfiya R. Masalimova, Alexey A. Chistyakov & Yusuf Ay** · *Frontiers in Education* · **2026-10-21** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R3 R4** · **DOI:** [10.3389/feduc.2026.1971476](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1971476) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1971476/full)

A three-level random-effects meta-analysis pooled 68 effect sizes from 58 independent studies. The mean learning/cognitive/linguistic effect favored GenAI (Hedges' g=0.864, 95% CI [0.720, 1.009]), but I² was about 88% and the 95% prediction interval [-0.213, 1.942] included no benefit. Funnel asymmetry and PET-PEESE sensitivity analyses indicated possible small-study inflation (roughly g=0.26–0.45 under those assumptions). **The mean is not a forecast for a new classroom.**

**Caveat:** Study outcomes mix language, knowledge and cognition; some designs are one-group pre/post; moderator signals did not survive conservative multiplicity adjustment. This synthesis cannot establish long-term independent skill retention.

### 2. Metacognitive Scaffolds Create Productive Friction for AI-Assisted Knowledge Work

**Viktor Kewenig, Lev Tankelevitch, Sean Rintel, Advait Sarkar & Abigail Sellen** · *arXiv preprint* · **2026-10-22** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R4** · **DOI:** not assigned / 未确认 · [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30945)

In a randomized between-subjects writing experiment (297 knowledge workers, 594 essays), dynamic prompts for goal specification and task decomposition improved essay quality. The quality advantage persisted in a short-lag task after the scaffolds were removed; topic recall was better three days later. Participants nevertheless rated the scaffolded AI as less helpful and more cognitively demanding. This is a concrete **productive-friction** signal: perceived convenience and measurable cognition can move in opposite directions.

**Caveat:** Unreviewed preprint, brief task sequence and three-day recall; not evidence of months-long skill development. The intervention bundles multiple scaffold components and does not isolate their individual causal contributions.

### 3. Towards Human-AI Complementarity for AI Safety

**Sander Lanbo Shi et al.** · *arXiv preprint, revised 2026-10-27* · **2026-10-18** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R2 R5 R8** · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2610.24969](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.24969) · [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24969)

The authors assembled 3,957 safety-relevant instances across eight datasets and six domains and collected 35,613 judgments from 416 human raters. Human and AI errors offered theoretical room for complementarity, yet tested assistance methods made people **less likely to correct AI mistakes**; tested hybridizations added little over the better single source on average. The paper was originally submitted October 18 and revised October 27; a revision is not a new publication.

**Caveat:** Unreviewed preprint with task- and assistance-specific conditions; the results do not rule out other routing or challenge mechanisms, nor measure long-term learning.

### 4. Applications of Generative Artificial Intelligence Teaching in Nursing Education: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials

**Qing Wan, Xisha Long, Tao Lin, Zheng Zhang & Yichen Meng** · *Nurse Educator (online ahead of print)* · **2026-10-23** · **NEW** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R3 R4** · **DOI:** [10.1097/NNE.0000000000002355](https://doi.org/10.1097/NNE.0000000000002355) · [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42924599/)

A systematic review/meta-analysis of 17 randomized studies (1,456 participants) found positive pooled associations for knowledge (SMD 0.53, 95% CI [0.03, 1.04]), practice-related performance (SMD 1.25, 95% CI [0.23, 2.28]) and teaching satisfaction (SMD 0.58, 95% CI [0.02, 1.13]). Attitudes, competency and self-efficacy did **not** significantly differ. This counterweights blanket claims that every learner outcome improves.

**Caveat:** Extreme heterogeneity and sensitivity to individual trials substantially limit confidence in magnitude and replicability. Clinical performance in supervised teaching is not post-AI independent professional competence.

### 5. Within-person reciprocal processes between AI feedback literacy and agentic engagement: a three-wave random-intercept cross-lagged panel study

**Shuai Xu** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-22** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R6** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1893874](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1893874) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1893874/full)

A three-wave, six-week-interval panel followed 480 Chinese undergraduates (399 complete cases). Within-person deviations in AI feedback literacy predicted later agentic engagement (β=0.222); engagement predicted later feedback literacy (β=0.263). The random-intercept model separates stable between-person differences from within-person change better than a conventional cross-lagged model.

**Caveat:** Observational self-reports and three waves over one semester; reciprocal prediction is not causal identification and is not objective measurement of durable epistemic conduct.

### 6. Scaffolding Generative AI as a Tutor: A Quasi-Experimental Study of Learning Outcomes and Motivational, Cognitive and Metacognitive Processes

**Chrysanthi Melanou & Maik Beege** · *Education Sciences* · **2026-04-20** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R4** · **DOI:** [10.3390/educsci16040651](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16040651) · [Primary record](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/16/4/651)

A four-hour quasi-experiment with 175 first-semester students compared full, light and no additional prompting scaffold. Knowledge, critical-thinking and reflective-use measures improved across groups, but **scaffolding intensity did not consistently differentiate outcomes**. This is important counterevidence to a simplistic rule that adding more prompts necessarily helps.

**Caveat:** One session, non-randomized group assignment, incomplete matched assessments, and possible ceiling/implicit AI-tutor scaffolding. The null comparison does not prove all metacognitive prompts ineffective. This study was published in April, not this week. Consensus fetched record ID 0a3333c6cf355da0991004d39707e3bd.

### 7. Human diversity fuels collective creativity that large language models cannot simulate or sustain

**Mengchen Dong & Hiromu Yakura** · *arXiv preprint* · **2026-07-29** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R3 R5 R7 R8** · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2607.26899](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.26899) · [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26899)

A preregistered creative-metaphor experiment compared native and non-native English writers under no-AI, AI-ideation and AI-refinement workflows. AI ideation compressed between-person diversity and erased a detectable non-native-writer diversity advantage; AI refinement preserved both. Simulated persona pools across three model families did not reproduce the diversity of the human pools. Individual output ratings could improve while collective diversity fell.

**Caveat:** Unreviewed preprint, specialized metaphor task and model/prompt choices; not proof that all AI use reduces cultural diversity or that persona simulation never works.

### 8. Dynamics of collective creativity in AI art competitions

**Mason Youngblood, Jeff Nusz & Joel Simon** · *arXiv preprint* · **2026-05-16** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R3 R7 R8 R9** · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2605.17141](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.17141) · [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17141)

An observational study of 130,882 Artbreeder images from 368 remix parties over 13 months found that branching human–AI image lineages tended toward simpler, recurring thematic attractors. Novel and complex parent images produced novel offspring that attracted likes, yet users preferentially remixed less novel, less complex images. Larger parties yielded more novelty but lower complexity. This is an ecological **selection-and-transmission** signal rather than a controlled causal test.

**Caveat:** Platform-specific self-selection, nonexperimental data, image-metric operationalization and no independent human-only counterfactual; the study cannot establish an AI-caused population-wide homogenization effect.

## Convergence, counterevidence and uncertainty

**Meta-analytic benefit is conditional.** The 58-study synthesis has a strongly positive average, but its prediction interval spans negative effects and small-study corrections shrink the estimate. The nursing review likewise reports favorable knowledge/practice outcomes with extreme heterogeneity and null results on other constructs. These findings do not overturn the Baseline distinction between assisted performance and independent retention.

**Productive friction is not equivalent to maximal friction.** Kewenig et al. find improved short-lag writing and recall with dynamic scaffolds, despite greater burden. Melanou and Beege find no consistent dose advantage from more elaborate prompting scaffolds. Different samples, tasks, designs and outcomes prevent a pooled conclusion. The relevant unresolved mechanism is what kind of effort preserves which capability, not whether every added prompt helps.

**Complementarity requires corrective behavior.** Shi et al. document that some assistance can raise human accuracy while reducing the rate at which humans catch AI mistakes. The team outcome must be compared with both solo baselines, not just with the unassisted human.

**Mean performance and collective variance are different estimands.** Dong and Yakura show workflow-dependent diversity compression in a controlled metaphor task; Youngblood et al. describe selection and attractor dynamics in a real creative platform. The latter is ecological but observational; neither proves long-run cultural homogenization across populations.
