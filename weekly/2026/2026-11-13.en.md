## Executive judgment

Two different designs converge on a bounded concern: the amount of AI assistance and the user's ability to monitor their own thinking should be measured separately. A three-wave survey of medical students associates AI dependence with lower self-reported critical thinking and clinical reasoning, with AI literacy moderating the association. A newly discovered four-experiment paper finds task-level metacognitive disengagement under some high-assistance and offloading conditions, with task complexity and AI literacy as moderators. Neither establishes lasting professional deskilling. The experimental work offers more direct task-level evidence than the survey, but generalization across settings and durations remains uncertain.

## Verified evidence

### 1. Association between generative AI dependence and medical students’ clinical reasoning: the statistical mediating role of critical thinking and the moderating role of AI literacy

**Guofang Wei & Yanling Wang** · *Frontiers in Medicine* · **2026-11-06** · **NEW** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R5** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fmed.2026.1994028](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1994028) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1994028/full)

A three-wave time-lagged survey of **648 medical students** found that AI dependence was negatively associated with critical thinking (standardized β = −0.379) and clinical reasoning (β = −0.320). AI literacy moderated the dependence–critical-thinking association (β = 0.168). The reported indirect association via critical thinking was −0.125 (95% CI [−0.165, −0.090]). **These are statistical pathways, not identified causal mechanisms.**

**Caveat:** Observational self-report measures and time-separated measurement cannot establish that AI dependence caused lower clinical reasoning or that independently tested diagnostic competence declined. The population is medical students, not practicing clinicians.

**Why it matters:** Even in a high-stakes domain, assisted work, perceived dependence and independently demonstrated competence require distinct measures.

### 2. Metacognitive disengagement and knowledge innovation quality in human–AI collaboration: the mediating role of cognitive offloading and the moderating roles of task complexity and AI literacy

**Wei Xiao, Cheng-Hao Men & Jia-Cong Zhang** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-28** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R5** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975833](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975833) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975833/full)

Across four task-level experiments, greater AI assistance increased measured metacognitive disengagement; cognitive offloading partly mediated this relation. A manipulation of offloading in a subsequent experiment was associated with poorer rated novelty, usefulness and developability of knowledge-innovation work. Higher task complexity strengthened the modeled indirect pathway; higher AI literacy weakened it.

**Caveat:** Context-specific experimental tasks and constructed innovation ratings do not establish long-term deterioration of metacognitive capacity, nor a universal effect of AI assistance. Mediation interpretations still depend on model assumptions.

**Why it matters:** This is a stronger task-level mechanism probe than a cross-sectional association, while still leaving the durability and transfer questions open.

## Evidence convergence and counterevidence

Both items distinguish AI use frequency from how cognitive operations are delegated. The four-experiment findings warrant more attention to task-level monitoring and literacy; the medical survey is consistent with, but cannot causally corroborate, that mechanism. The current Baseline already records that bounded assistance can be beneficial and that human–AI synergy is conditional. These studies do not justify a blanket claim that AI use harms cognition. No fresh verified evidence was selected for R7–R9; this is a coverage result, not evidence of no activity in those fields.
