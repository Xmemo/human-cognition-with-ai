## Executive judgment

The most recent advance is a formal analysis of human–AI complementarity: synergy requires effective error correction, not simply two individually accurate agents. A newly discovered randomized clinician study complicates the interface-design story: adding AI confidence scores or cited explanations did not measurably increase trust, and reported confidence diverged from actual behavioral influence. Other studies reveal small reciprocal associations between AI use and self-efficacy, task-specific creative effects during AI generation waits, and the need to distinguish information visibility from social imitation.

**Baseline: NO_CHANGE.** These results sharpen the current Control / Retention / Variance view, but do not establish long-term unaided skill retention or durable real-world cultural change.

## Verified evidence

### 1. Transparency features alone are insufficient for trust calibration in artificial intelligence diagnostic support

**Frank, Stevens, Hsu, Gao, Streiff & Engineer** · *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association* · **2026-10-14** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[A]** randomized narrow outcome · **Radars: R2 R5 R6** · **DOI:** [10.1093/jamia/ocag176](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag176) · [PubMed primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872605/)

**220 clinicians** were randomized to simulated diagnostic assistance without transparency, with numeric AI confidence, or with narrative explanations and citations. Neither transparency condition measurably improved tool-specific trust, global trust, or perceived credibility. Any AI assistance increased confidence by about 6.7%; narrative explanations affected confidence more, while the no-transparency group showed greater diagnostic-revision influence. **Negative result:** transparency widgets are not sufficient for trust calibration.

**Caveat:** short simulated cases, self-reported trust, and no patient outcomes or long-term behavioral follow-up. [A] applies only to the randomized vignette comparison.

### 2. When Do Humans and Machines Truly Complement Each Other? Formal Conditions and Empirical Evidence for Human-AI Synergy

**Andrea Campagner, Federico Cabitza & Vincenzo Crupi** · *arXiv* · **2026-10-20** · **NEW** · **[C]** formal model and seven clinical case analyses · **Radars: R5 R8** · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2610.27965](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.27965) · [Canonical preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27965)

The paper derives mathematical conditions for synergy from patterns of joint correctness. Each partner must be capable of **recognizing and correcting the other's mistakes**. Seven clinical cases illustrate a possible **dominance trap**: an apparently superior algorithm can suppress human corrective intervention and prevent team performance from exceeding the better agent.

**Caveat:** **under peer review**; formal assumptions and retrospective cases do not constitute a randomized intervention or a universal estimate of human–AI synergy.

### 3. How generative AI becomes embedded in design learning: a 14-instructional-day intensive longitudinal study of reciprocal dynamics between AI use and self-efficacy

**Yang Li, Yi Yang & Silu Bao** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-14** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** intensive diary associations · **Radars: R1 R2 R3** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975841](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975841) · [Publisher](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1975841/full)

**260 design undergraduates**, **3,558 person-days**, 14 instructional days. Higher-than-usual AI use predicted slightly higher next-instructional-day design-learning self-efficacy (β=0.043); self-efficacy also predicted next-day AI use (β=0.059). Greater baseline hassles accompanied more AI use and lower average self-efficacy.

**Caveat:** observational cross-lagged self-reports; no objectively tested independent skill, causal assignment, or long-term transfer. Confidence in learning is not learning retention.

### 4. Behavioral differentiation during the AIGC image generation waiting period: effects on creativity in conceptual design

**Ziliang Zhu & Jiang Xu** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-12** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** task-specific experiments · **Radars: R3 R4 R5** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1924155](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1924155) · [Publisher](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1924155/full)

Three experiments with design students (**N=30, 24, 60**) classified AI image-generation waiting behaviors, measured workload, and compared four assigned behaviors. One condition scored below the other three on immediate creativity; those three did not significantly differ. The cognitive unit of analysis extends **between** prompt submission and AI output.

**Caveat:** fixed 120-second waits, one immediate design task, and small student samples; the study does not establish a universal incubation strategy or durable creativity improvement.

### 5. Novice Reliance Calibration in AI-Assisted Decision Making: The Role of Explanations and Self-Assessment

**Eun Jeong Kang, Peter Duan & Swati Mishra** · *arXiv* · **2026-10-06** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** bounded behavioral preprint · **Radars: R2 R4 R5** · **DOI:** [10.48550/arXiv.2610.07800](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.07800) · [Canonical preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07800)

In a between-subjects study of **110 novices** performing clinical entity extraction with limited feedback, explanations accompanied drift toward **over-reliance**. Higher self-assessed task understanding was associated with more selective reliance. This qualifies claims that explanation alone necessarily improves decision quality.

**Caveat:** **under review**, narrow task, novice sample, and no actual clinical decisions. Self-rated understanding does not prove expertise.

### 6. Augmentation or Erosion? Regulating Cognitive Offloading in Generative AI-Supported STEM Learning

**Daiki Nakamura** · *Educational Psychology Review* · **2026-10-01** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[C]** conceptual synthesis · **Radars: R1 R2 R4** · **DOI:** [10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6) · [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6)

The article distinguishes operations **peripheral to** versus **constitutive of** a specific learning goal. Offloading the former may free resources; bypassing the latter may erode later unaided capability unless learners reconstruct the reasoning. Seven conditioned propositions and measurement criteria make this a useful theoretical refinement of the offloading problem.

**Caveat:** peer-reviewed **theory, not a new experiment**. The offloading-regulation construct and its predictions require direct empirical validation.

### 7. Artificial intelligence systems distort upstream selection in human social learning

**William J. Brady, Chen-Wei Yu, Nicholas Ornstein, Bolun Sun & Joshua Conrad Jackson** · *Current Opinion in Psychology* · **2026-09-09** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[C]** conceptual synthesis · **Radars: R7 R8 R9** · **DOI:** [10.1016/j.copsyc.2026.102412](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copsyc.2026.102412) · [PubMed primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42715802/)

The authors separate **upstream selection** (which information is visible) from **downstream selection** (which visible examples people copy). Engagement-optimized feeds may overexpose extremes; generative systems may privilege typical answers while hiding alternatives. Both could distort apparent prevalence and prestige in social learning.

**Caveat:** a proposed explanatory framework, **not** direct longitudinal evidence that AI has already homogenized real populations. Consensus record fetched and cross-checked against the canonical publication record.

## Convergence, counterevidence and uncertainty

The new formal complementarity paper agrees with prior meta-analytic cautions that human + AI does not automatically outperform the best component. The clinician RCT provides a **null trust result** despite changes in confidence and diagnostic behavior. The novice preprint reports an over-reliance risk under explanations. Their samples and outcomes differ, so neither should be treated as a pooled universal explanation effect.

The diary result concerns **self-efficacy**, not independently demonstrated skill. The design-wait experiment concerns **immediate creativity**, not durable learning. The upstream-selection argument concerns a **plausible mechanism**, not established long-run cultural feedback. R7–R9 did not yield a new verified multigeneration real-world persistence experiment this week.
