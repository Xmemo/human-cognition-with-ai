## 1｜NEW — Over-reliance is better treated as a person–environment system than a single user trait

### The dependency trap: a systematic review of the contributing factors and consequences of student over-reliance on generative AI in higher education

**Authors:** Chenhui An, Ke Lu, Junfeng Yang  
**Source:** *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* · **2026-09-04**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R1 R2 R4 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-08959-2

This systematic review synthesizes **54 empirical studies** on student over-reliance on generative AI. It groups contributing conditions into psychological factors, capability gaps, environmental stress, and technological pull, and groups reported consequences into cognitive, learning-efficacy, academic-integrity, and psychosocial domains.

**Why it matters:** the useful shift is away from treating dependence as a binary property of the learner. The review frames over-reliance as emerging from person–environment interaction and proposes a possible self-reinforcing loop in which consequences can increase the conditions for further reliance.

**Caveat:** the proposed "dependency trap" is a review-level conceptual synthesis, not a longitudinal causal mechanism demonstrated by the included studies. It therefore reinforces G01/G03 but does not resolve them.

## 2｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — A systematic review converges on calibrated delegation, but long-term causal evidence is still scarce

### Between cognitive offloading and critical autonomy: a systematic review of the epistemic implications of generative AI in higher education

**Authors:** Iván Claudio Suazo Galdames, Meylin Santiesteban Velázquez, Mahia Saracostti, Alain Manuel Chaple Gil  
**Source:** *Frontiers in Education* · **2026-09-03**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R1 R2 R3 R4`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1878665

The PRISMA review includes **26 empirical studies**. Across a heterogeneous literature, guided and verification-oriented uses of GenAI were associated with feedback, conceptual clarification, reflection, and self-regulated learning, whereas uncritical or convenience-driven use was repeatedly associated with reduced source checking, cognitive offloading, automation bias, and weaker evaluative engagement.

**Why it matters:** this independently converges with the project's current view that the relevant question is not simply whether cognition is offloaded, but **which epistemic functions are delegated and which remain under human judgment**.

**Caveat:** much of the underlying literature is cross-sectional, exploratory, or self-reported. The review explicitly does not establish long-term cognitive decline or a causal benefit of any single scaffolding component.

## 3｜NEW — Technical vocabulary can hide a poor mental model of ChatGPT

### Behind the screen: laypersons' mental models about ChatGPT

**Authors:** Maria Schneller, Jonas Seier, Stefan E. Huber, Iliana Loi, Dietrich Albert  
**Source:** *Frontiers in Computer Science* · **2026-09-07**  
**Evidence:** **[C] Conceptual / exploratory** · **Radars:** `R2 R4 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1915035

Semi-structured interviews with **21 technical laypersons** found fragmented mental models combining deterministic, probabilistic, anthropomorphic, and dynamic conceptions of ChatGPT. Every participant expressed at least one misconception. Among participants who used technical terminology, some could reproduce the right vocabulary while giving an incompatible mechanism-level explanation — what the authors call "chauffeur knowledge."

**Why it matters:** AI literacy should not be inferred from whether a person knows terms such as "LLM," "probability," or "hallucination." Functional understanding may matter more for deciding when to verify, distrust, or reinterpret model output.

**Caveat:** this is a small qualitative convenience sample. It identifies configurations and measurement problems, not population prevalence or causal links to over-reliance.

## 4｜NEW — AI authorship bias depends on how deeply the reader understands the text

### Text comprehension moderates cognitive biases toward AI authorship in literary content

**Authors:** S. Raglio, F. Miriani, M. Focareta, S. Rebora, M. Rospocher, S. G. Chiarella, D. M. Gagliardi, M. Salgaro, G. Torromino  
**Source:** *Scientific Reports* · **2026-09-08**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R3 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-65454-z

Participants rated literary texts that were labelled as human-made, AI-made, or human+AI-made. The reported authorship bias was **bidirectionally moderated by comprehension**: participants with lower comprehension showed a negative bias toward AI-labelled texts, while those with higher comprehension showed a positive bias.

**Why it matters:** judgments about AI authorship are not simply properties of the artifact or the label. The reader's level of engagement and understanding can change the direction of the bias, complicating simple claims that audiences inherently devalue AI-associated work.

**Caveat:** the study concerns evaluative judgments about literary texts. It does not establish psychological ownership by creators, legal authorship, or general attitudes toward all AI-generated content.

## 5｜NEW — Workplace human–AI interaction is multidimensional, and its performance link is partly psychological

### Dancing with AI: how human-AI interaction affects employee task performance

**Authors:** Yepeng Wu, Yuanyuan Jiao, Ping Li, Yujie Liang  
**Source:** *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* · **2026-09-09**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R2 R5 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-09043-5

Using grounded theory, scale development, and workplace survey data, the study models human–AI interaction through three dimensions: **anthropomorphic tool use, adaptive trust, and unidirectional emotional connection**. Higher measured human–AI interaction was associated with better task performance, with role identity and self-efficacy acting as partial mediators.

**Why it matters:** workplace collaboration is not captured well by a single "AI use" variable. Trust calibration, self-concept, and the perceived relationship to the system may be part of the pathway linking AI use to work outcomes.

**Caveat:** the performance mechanism is based on observational survey relationships, not an experiment. The study should not be read as evidence that increasing anthropomorphism or emotional connection will causally improve performance.

## 6｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — AI proportion changes not just how fast groups converge, but what their shared conventions become

### AI agents reshape consensus formation in human groups

**Authors:** Lin Chen, Ziyi Liu, Xia Hu, Yong Li  
**Source:** arXiv:2609.02122 · **2026-09-02**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R5 R7 R8 R9`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.02122

In a repeated collaborative description game, the researchers varied LLM-agent proportions across mixed groups. The relationship was non-linear: low agent proportions facilitated human-led consensus, intermediate proportions disrupted convergence, and high proportions restored strong convergence while shifting the emerging convention toward agent-led semantics.

The content changed as well as the convergence rate. Human-led conventions were more concrete and analogy-based; agent-led conventions were more abstract and geometrically segmented. The authors attribute agent influence partly to shared linguistic priors across models and stable agent expression, while humans initially resisted partners perceived as AI but increasingly conformed as agent presence grew.

**Why it matters:** this substantially narrows G05. **Agent proportion is itself a network-design variable**, and its effect can cross qualitative regimes rather than scale monotonically. Hybrid collective intelligence therefore cannot be reduced to adding more high-performing AI participants.

**Caveat:** this is a preprint using a controlled convention-formation game. It does not establish long-term cultural change in organizations or society, so G06 remains open.

## 7｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — Explanations can improve human mental models in a real autonomous system

### Explainable deep learning improves human mental models of self-driving cars

**Authors:** Eoin M. Kenny et al.  
**Source:** *Nature* · **2026-09-02** · volume 657, 114–120  
**Evidence:** **[A] Strong empirical** · **Radars:** `R2 R4 R5 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10950-5

The authors introduce a Concept-Wrapper Network that grounds a self-driving planner's decisions in human-interpretable concepts without materially reducing driving performance. They test it through real-vehicle deployment, expert and non-expert studies, and public-road data from Las Vegas.

In follow-up studies, explanations improved participants' mental models and their ability to predict vehicle behavior. In a 99-person public-road video study, explanations produced large improvements in perception and comprehension and a medium improvement in projection for surprising events, while not improving routine unsurprising events.

**Why it matters:** this provides unusually strong ecological evidence for a central Tools-for-Thought principle: **an AI system can improve human performance by improving the human's model of the AI**, rather than by taking over more of the task. It is a concrete example of augmentation through calibrated understanding.

**Caveat:** this concerns safety-critical autonomous driving, not generative-AI learning or long-term skill retention. It supports a mechanism around mental models and appropriate reliance, not a broad claim about cognitive resilience.

# Evidence convergence

### 1. The field is moving from "AI use" toward representations and control states

The two systematic reviews, the ChatGPT mental-model study, and the real-world explainability study converge on a more precise unit of analysis: **what the human believes the system can do, which functions are delegated, and whether those beliefs are calibrated to the system's actual limits**. This is more informative than counting usage frequency alone.

### 2. Structure matters at both the individual and collective level

At the individual level, pedagogical structure and mental models affect how AI is used. At the collective level, the new mixed-group experiment shows that **composition itself changes the dynamics and semantics of consensus**. "Interaction architecture" therefore increasingly spans workflow design, user cognition, and network composition.

### 3. Better measured outcomes still do not settle retained capability

This week's workplace and explainability studies show benefits in task performance, mental models, prediction, or situational awareness. None measures what remains after months or years of AI use, and none closes the AI-on → AI-off transfer gap. G01 remains the most important longitudinal limitation on strong claims about cognitive change.

# Conclusions not supported

- The new dependency review does not prove that AI dependence is a self-reinforcing causal loop.
- Knowing AI terminology does not prove functional understanding, but the small interview study does not establish how common specific misconceptions are.
- Positive workplace associations do not establish that anthropomorphism or emotional connection causes better performance.
- AI-labelled literary work is not universally devalued or preferred; the observed direction depended on comprehension in this study.
- The mixed-group consensus experiment does not establish a universal optimal AI proportion.
- Explainable autonomous driving does not prove that explanations always increase trust or improve long-term learning.

# Open Questions & Research Gaps

### Newly exposed

- None strong enough for a new long-lived gap ID this week.

### Persistent

- **G01** — retained capability after months or years of sustained AI use remains unresolved.
- **G02** — long-term expert cognition remains unresolved; this week's evidence is not an expert longitudinal study.
- **G03** — systematic reviews continue to support structured and verification-oriented use, but the active causal component remains unresolved.
- **G04** — the new mental-model study exposes weaknesses in terminology-based AI literacy measures, but durable real-world epistemic behavior remains untested.
- **G06** — the new consensus experiment remains a short controlled task, so persistence of Machine Culture feedback loops in real social systems remains unresolved.

### Narrowed / closed

- **G05 narrowed again, not closed.** The mixed human–AI consensus experiment adds **agent proportion / group composition** as a concrete structural variable and shows that its effects can be non-linear. This extends last week's error-correlation mechanism: hybrid performance depends not only on who makes different errors, but also on how many artificial agents participate and how their shared priors alter group convergence and convention content.
- No gap is closed this week.
