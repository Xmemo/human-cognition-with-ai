## 1｜NEW — Personality pairing can causally change human–AI team performance

### Personality pairing improves human-AI collaboration

**Authors:** Harang Ju, Sinan Aral  
**Source:** *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* · version of record **2026-08-28**  
**Evidence:** **[A] Strong empirical** · **Radars:** `R5`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2530627123

A preregistered randomized experiment paired **1,258 U.S. participants** with AI agents prompted to exhibit different Big Five personality traits. The teams produced 7,266 display ads; outputs were independently rated and then tested in a field campaign on X with nearly five million impressions.

Different human–AI personality pairings produced meaningfully different teamwork, productivity, and quality outcomes. The effects were not a simple “matching is always better” rule: some combinations improved one modality or outcome while harming another.

**Why it matters:** personalization is becoming an experimentally manipulable interaction variable. “The same model for everyone” may hide substantial heterogeneity in human–AI performance.

**Caveat:** the task was advertising production in a short collaboration session. The findings should not yet be generalized to long-term cognitive development or unrelated domains.

## 2｜NEW — LLMs know average social norms surprisingly well, but their errors are correlated

### Large language models outperform humans at estimating society’s everyday norms

**Authors:** Kimmo Eriksson, Simon Karlsson, Irina Vartanova, Pontus Strimling  
**Source:** *Communications AI & Computing* · **2026-09-01**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R5 R8 R9`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s44488-026-00018-8

Six LLMs and **320 U.S. participants** estimated population-average appropriateness ratings for **555 everyday scenarios**. The strongest LLMs were substantially more accurate than individual humans.

The collective result was more important than the solo ranking. Human errors were comparatively independent, so aggregating people sharply improved accuracy. LLM errors were much more correlated across models, so model-only aggregation helped little. A human–LLM hybrid produced the best estimates; one reported hybrid reduced error by **32.5% relative to Gemini 3 Flash alone** and by **15.9% relative to the human aggregate alone**.

**Why it matters:** human diversity can remain valuable even when the average individual human is weaker than the model. Complementarity may come from **different error structure**, not merely from combining two high-performing agents.

**Caveat:** the hypotheses were not preregistered, the benchmark concerns U.S. population-average norms, and the study measures norm estimation rather than real-time norm-following or long-run cultural dynamics.

## 3｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — Structured AI-supported writing can improve both writing and critical-AI practice

### From tool to scaffold: structured human–AI collaboration and its effects on academic writing and digital critical thinking among Saudi EFL learners

**Authors:** O. Alshehri, Ali Lamouchi, M. Abdellatif, M. Al-Dosari, M. Mekheimer, Mohamed Ali Nemt-allah  
**Source:** *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-05-26**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R2 R3 R4 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1830103

In a small 10–11 week quasi-experiment, 53 Saudi university EFL students received either conventional instruction or a structured AI-supported workflow combining problem framing, iterative drafting, source/citation verification, and responsible-use regulation. The AI-supported group improved more on writing and a digital critical-thinking scale.

**Why it matters:** the study adds longer-than-one-session evidence that AI can be embedded as a scaffold while keeping verification and responsibility explicit.

**Caveat:** there were only two intact classes, no individual randomization, a single institution, and several intervention components changed together. The unusually large reported effects should be treated as preliminary.

## 4｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — Group + AI can outperform both individual + AI and human-only groups in a creativity task

### Group-AI Collaboration Enhances Creativity Performance: The Roles of Perspective-Taking and AI Utilisation Strategies

**Authors:** Zhihao Zhou, Letao Xiao, Jiayin Wang, Shuai Tong, Wenyu Zhang, Ning Hao  
**Source:** *Journal of Computer Assisted Learning* · **2026-03-09**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R3 R5 R8`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70223

Ninety-six university students completed a scientific divergent-use task under group-AI, individual-AI, and human-only group conditions. Group-AI collaboration produced the strongest usefulness, originality, and quality scores. Behavioral coding suggested that perspective taking, strategic AI use, and selective integration of AI and peer ideas were important mediators.

**Why it matters:** AI does not inevitably reduce collective creative performance. Group composition and selective integration can change the direction of the effect.

**Caveat:** this was a controlled university task, not a long-running creative team or population-level cultural system. It does not by itself establish preservation of long-run between-person variance.

## 5｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — “More structure” is not automatically better

### Scaffolding Human-AI Collaboration: A Field Experiment on Behavioral Protocols and Cognitive Reframing

**Authors:** Alex Farach, Alexia Cambon, Lev Tankelevitch, Connie Hsueh, Rebecca Janssen  
**Source:** arXiv:2604.08678 · **2026-04-09** · field experiment with 388 employees  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R4 R5`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.08678

All participants had access to the same AI tool, but the surrounding scaffolding differed. A mandated behavioral protocol for pair work produced more friction, lower document production, and lower measured quality than naturalistic use. A separate cognitive reframing intervention that positioned AI as a thought partner showed more favorable patterns at the top of the quality distribution.

**Why it matters:** this is useful counterevidence against turning “structured AI use” into a universal recipe. **Interaction design has implementation costs.** A scaffold can preserve agency in one context and create coordination friction in another.

**Caveat:** the authors report session-time confounding, differential attrition, ceiling effects, and sensitivity to LLM-based grading. The result is informative about mechanism and implementation, not a clean causal ranking of all scaffolding strategies.

# Evidence convergence

### 1. Interaction architecture is becoming more granular

The field is moving beyond “AI vs no AI” and even beyond “human-led vs model-led.” This week adds at least three further variables: **person–agent fit, scaffold type, and implementation friction**. The defensible conclusion is not that one interaction pattern is universally best, but that AI effects are conditional on how the human–AI system is composed and operated.

### 2. Human advantage can survive as diversity even when individual performance does not

The new social-norm study provides a concrete mechanism for the project’s `Variance` dimension. Individual humans were weaker estimators than strong LLMs, yet their errors were less correlated. That diversity became useful under aggregation and improved the best human–LLM hybrid.

### 3. Structured support remains promising, but G03 becomes more important rather than less

The Saudi writing study supports verification-rich, responsibility-oriented scaffolding. The workplace field experiment simultaneously shows that a rigid behavioral protocol can create a substantial friction tax. Together they argue for **component-level tests**, not the slogan “more scaffolding is better.”

# Conclusions not supported

- Personality matching does not establish one universal “best AI personality.”
- Better LLM estimates of average norms do not mean LLMs possess superior moral judgment or can replace human social deliberation.
- Hybrid advantage in a norm-estimation benchmark does not establish a general optimal human–AI network topology.
- The structured-writing study does not establish long-term retained capability after AI removal.
- The group-creativity experiment does not refute population-level homogenization risks in other settings.

# Open Questions & Research Gaps

### Newly exposed

- None strong enough for a new long-lived gap ID this week.

### Persistent

- **G01** — months/years-long retained capability remains unresolved.
- **G02** — long-term expert cognition under sustained AI use remains unresolved.
- **G03** — the active ingredient in beneficial AI scaffolding remains unresolved; this week’s contrasting interventions make the mechanism question more important.
- **G04** — short scales and bounded behavioral tasks still do not establish durable real-world epistemic agency.
- **G05** — hybrid structures that preserve useful human diversity remain incompletely mapped.
- **G06** — real-world persistence of Machine Culture feedback loops remains unresolved.

### Narrowed / closed

- **G05 narrowed, not closed.** The new social-norm study provides direct evidence that **error correlation** is one mechanism through which human diversity can add value to otherwise stronger models: model errors were correlated, human errors were more independent, and hybrid aggregation performed best. This answers one piece of the mechanism question but not general network design, cultural persistence, or collective variance over time.
- No gap is closed this week.
