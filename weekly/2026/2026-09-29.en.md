## 1｜NEW — The direction of evaluative judgment matters after AI is removed

### Telling Students to Evaluate Does Not Make It Happen: Task Stage, Offloading Tendency, and Error Detection in AI-Assisted Student Writing

**Authors:** Mengmeng Fan, Pengcheng Chang  
**Source:** *Behavioral Sciences* · **2026-09-17**  
**Evidence:** **[A] Strong empirical** · **Radars:** `R1 R2 R3 R4`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16091671

A four-arm randomized experiment at Site A assigned 744 undergraduates to different human–AI divisions of labor across four rounds of report writing; 639 completed. A reduced replication at Site B randomized 126 students; 109 completed. At the Week 6 post-test, **all AI was withdrawn**.

Two arrangements in which students had to judge AI-produced text outperformed the “student drafts, AI evaluates” arrangement and the no-AI control on independent transfer (reported d ≈ 0.35–0.36). But error detection did not follow the same pattern: delegating revision could preserve transfer while failing to preserve error-detection performance.

The process logs add an important boundary condition. Students higher in dependent-offloading tendency were more likely to re-outsource the evaluative judgment they had been assigned, and subjective satisfaction did not predict error-detection performance.

**Why it matters:** this is unusually direct evidence that **which cognitive stage is delegated matters**, and that “keeping a human in the loop” is too coarse a description. It also shows that retained writing performance and retained error-detection skill are separable outcomes.

**Caveat:** the four conditions do not orthogonally isolate every component. The authors explicitly note that the transfer advantage identifies the **direction of judgment** more cleanly than a single causal micro-mechanism. This sharpens G03 but does not close it.

## 2｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — A semester-long study finds no automatic learning advantage from AI integration

### Generative AI and Learning Dynamics in Higher Education: A Longitudinal Empirical Study

**Authors:** Chrysanthi Melanou, Maik Beege, Martin Kimmig  
**Source:** *Journal of Computer Assisted Learning* · **2026-09-10**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R1 R2 R4`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/jcal.70322

Across a semester, 87 business-informatics students in three parallel classes experienced tutor-guided AI, unguided AI, or no-AI instruction. All groups gained knowledge, but AI integration did **not** produce a statistically significant additional knowledge benefit. Critical thinking remained stable overall, while reflective AI use was higher in the AI condition and positively associated with critical thinking.

**Why it matters:** this extends the time horizon beyond one-session studies and again argues against treating AI as a static cognitive enhancer. The educational effect appears embedded in instructional framing and metacognitive regulation.

**Caveat:** this is a small quasi-experiment with intact classes, not randomized individual assignment, and it does not test delayed performance after months or years of AI use. G01 therefore remains open.

## 3｜NEW — Hybrid collaboration can improve performance without the situation-awareness loss seen in AI-led work

### Comparison of Human-AI Collaboration Modes in Cross-Platform Public Opinion Analysis: An Experimental Study on Analyst Performance and Cognitive Load

**Authors:** Lin Wang, Qian Huang, Kai Wang, Jiacheng Lou, Chunhong Yuan  
**Source:** *International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction* · **2026-09-15**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R2 R5 R6`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2026.2728615

A between-subjects experiment with 320 participants compared AI-led, human-led, and hybrid collaboration in cross-platform public-opinion analysis. Reported overall accuracy was highest in the hybrid condition (85.42%), ahead of AI-led (77.98%) and human-led (72.82%). AI-led work was more efficient but showed lower situation awareness; human-led work carried the highest cognitive load. The hybrid condition combined task allocation with bidirectional feedback and produced the strongest overall profile.

**Why it matters:** synergy is again a **division-of-labor problem**, not simply the arithmetic addition of a human and a model. Situation awareness is also a useful outcome because it captures what the human still understands while the system performs.

**Caveat:** this is one specialized analysis task and the paper does not establish a universal optimum for “hybrid” work. The exact allocation and interface structure remain part of the treatment.

## 4｜NEW — AI-assisted design changes the profile of ideation rather than uniformly improving creativity

### Generative AI as a cognitive support tool in early-stage design ideation: A human–AI co-ideation experiment

**Author:** Yu-Ju Lin  
**Source:** *PLOS ONE* · **2026-09-17**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R3 R4 R5`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0358542

Thirty-two undergraduate design students completed both AI-assisted and human-only ideation conditions in a counterbalanced within-subject experiment. After multiplicity correction, AI-assisted outputs scored higher on problem framing, mechanism, creator perspective, and evaluator perspective, whereas human-only outputs scored higher on innovation and proposed solution strategies. No robust difference emerged across the five preliminary-design refinement attributes.

**Why it matters:** “AI improves creativity” is too coarse. Different stages and dimensions of creative cognition can move in different directions, and subjective support does not imply a uniform originality advantage.

**Caveat:** the sample is small, student-based, and task-specific; the study does not establish durable creative-skill change.

## 5｜NEW — Evidence for children's creative development remains provisional and highly facilitation-dependent

### The role of generative AI in facilitating children's creative thinking and cognitive development: a systematic scoping review

**Authors:** Tianyue Niu, Huiying Liu, Patrick Pang, Yiming Taclis Luo, Ting Liu  
**Source:** *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-09-14**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R3 R4`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1880052

The JBI/PRISMA-ScR review mapped 24 sources, including 22 primary empirical studies. The literature reports possible benefits for divergent thinking, narrative creativity, and creative self-efficacy, but the authors emphasize substantial heterogeneity, geographic concentration, some non-peer-reviewed evidence, and the absence of a formal risk-of-bias appraisal.

**Why it matters:** the review expands the population beyond university students while reinforcing the same boundary: AI should be treated as a **conditional supplement** whose value depends on developmental fit, supervision, access, and task design.

**Caveat:** this is a scoping review rather than an efficacy meta-analysis. It does not justify claims that GenAI broadly improves children's cognitive development or democratizes creativity.

## 6｜NEW — Human–AI cooperation depends partly on whether the AI seems able to understand the human perspective

### Artificial Intelligence (AI) Can't Feel Me: Lower Perceived AI Perspective–Taking Impedes Human–AI Cooperation

**Authors:** Xuyao Wu, Ye Li, Ruining Wang, Man Hai  
**Source:** *European Journal of Social Psychology* · **2026-09-11**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R5 R8 R9`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.70112

Across five exploratory studies, participants were generally less willing to cooperate with AI holding partial decision authority. Perceived AI perspective-taking helped explain this reluctance: when an AI appeared to understand—or slightly overestimate—the perspective of a person facing unfairness, willingness to cooperate increased.

**Why it matters:** human–AI cooperation is not determined only by instrumental competence or outcome quality. Social-cognitive judgments about whether the artificial partner “gets” the human can alter cooperation.

**Caveat:** the studies were exploratory and not preregistered. The findings concern perceived perspective-taking and willingness to cooperate, not long-run trust calibration or collective norm formation.

## 7｜NEW — LLMs used as human proxies require claim-specific validity, not generic “human-likeness”

### Large language models as human proxies

**Authors:** Nikita Karetnikov, Iyad Rahwan, Davor Svetinovic  
**Source:** *Nature Computational Science* · **2026-09-18**  
**Evidence:** **[C] Conceptual / exploratory** · **Radars:** `R7 R8 R9`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-026-01060-3

The review organizes LLM-as-human-proxy research into four roles: believable agents, task agents, experimental subjects, and silicon samples. Its central methodological point is that “similarity to humans” is not one validity property. Each proxy role supports a different scientific claim and therefore requires different validation criteria.

**Why it matters:** this is directly relevant to Machine Behaviour and hybrid-society research. A model that predicts aggregate preferences, imitates dialogue, or substitutes for experimental participants cannot be treated as validated for all three purposes.

**Caveat:** this is a methodological review, not evidence that LLM proxies are generally valid or invalid.

## 8｜NEW — Critical-thinking associations differ by how students organize their AI use

### Associations between generative AI usage orientations and critical thinking disposition among college students in China

**Authors:** Shuai Yin, Yitao Gan, Qing Zhao  
**Source:** *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-09-28**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R1 R2 R3 R4`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1957029

A cross-sectional survey of 430 college students distinguished three usage orientations: **Tool**, **Guidance**, and **Collaboration**. After adjustment, Tool-oriented use showed a negative association with critical-thinking disposition, while Guidance and Collaboration showed positive associations; the orientation block added about 10.4 percentage points of explained variance.

**Why it matters:** this is another independent signal that **usage structure carries information beyond usage frequency**.

**Caveat:** all focal variables were measured at one time point, so the study does not establish temporal ordering or causality. It cannot tell us whether AI-use orientation changes critical thinking or whether students with different critical-thinking dispositions choose different orientations.

## 9｜NEWLY DISCOVERED — Prompt training changes what professional users get from GenAI ideation

### Human-GenAI Collaboration in Creative Ideation: A Study with Advertising Professionals

**Authors:** Isabella Carvalho Milano, Francine Zanin Bagatini, Marcelo Gattermann Perin, Lucia Salmonson Guimarães Barros  
**Source:** *Journal of Advertising Research* · **2026-09-01**  
**Evidence:** **[B] Promising / moderate** · **Radars:** `R2 R3 R5`  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00218499.2026.2702092

Ninety-eight advertising professionals were randomly assigned to no-GenAI, GenAI, or GenAI-after-prompt-training conditions. The prompt-trained GenAI group generated significantly more ideas than the other groups, while perceived creativity did not differ.

**Why it matters:** this adds a professional sample and shows that user training can materially change the outcome of AI-supported divergent ideation. It also cautions against treating a tool's effect as separable from user skill.

**Caveat:** the main advantage concerns idea quantity in a specific professional ideation task. It is not a longitudinal expert-cognition result and therefore does not resolve G02.

# Evidence convergence

### 1. “Interaction architecture” is becoming more granular

The strongest new evidence no longer stops at “structured use is better.” It distinguishes **which stage is delegated, who evaluates whose output, whether the human retains situation awareness, and whether the user has the skill to operate the collaboration pattern**.

### 2. Retained capability is multidimensional

The randomized writing study shows that later independent writing performance and later error detection can diverge. A single “transfer” score may therefore miss what exactly has been retained: production, evaluation, diagnosis, revision, or calibration.

### 3. AI-assisted creativity looks increasingly profile-shaped rather than uniformly higher or lower

Across the design experiment, children's scoping review, and professional ideation study, AI effects differ across idea quantity, framing, innovation, solution strategy, and user training. Mean output quality is not an adequate proxy for creative capability or population diversity.

### 4. Social cognition is part of human–AI system performance

Situation awareness, perceived perspective-taking, reflective use, and usage orientation all affect or correlate with outcomes. Human–AI performance is therefore partly a problem of **representation and control**, not just model capability.

# Conclusions not supported

- The new randomized writing study does **not** prove that one universal human–AI writing workflow is optimal.
- A semester-long null additional learning effect does **not** prove that GenAI has no educational value.
- Hybrid collaboration outperforming two alternatives in one public-opinion task does **not** prove hybrid systems always outperform humans or AI alone.
- The design-ideation study does **not** establish that AI reduces or improves creativity in general.
- The children's scoping review does **not** establish durable cognitive-development benefits.
- Perspective-taking effects do **not** show that AI actually understands human mental states.
- LLM human-proxy similarity in one role does **not** validate the model as a proxy for another role.
- Cross-sectional usage-orientation associations do **not** establish causal effects on critical thinking.

# Open Questions & Research Gaps

### Newly exposed

- **None strong enough for a new long-lived gap ID.**

### Persistent

- **G01 — retained capability over months/years:** the semester-long study lengthens the observation window, and the randomized writing study adds AI-off transfer, but neither establishes delayed months/years-long retained capability.
- **G02 — sustained expert cognition:** the advertising-professional experiment adds an expert/professional task context, but not a longitudinal expert trajectory.
- **G03 — active mechanism inside bounded support:** the randomized writing study makes the **direction of evaluative judgment** a concrete candidate mechanism, but its conditions still differ on multiple task components. The gap is sharper, not resolved.
- **G04 — durable behavioral validation of epistemic agency:** new usage-orientation and reflective-use findings remain largely survey/correlational outside the writing experiment.
- **G05 — human–AI network structures and collective variance:** no catch-up item supersedes the 2026-09-10 evidence on error correlation and agent proportion; topology/model heterogeneity remain unmapped.
- **G06 — persistence of Machine Culture feedback loops:** no new long-horizon real-world evidence closes the ecological/time-horizon gap.

### Narrowed / closed

- **No formal long-lived gap status change in this catch-up.** G03 receives an important mechanism-level signal, but the evidence is not yet clean enough to mark the ledger as narrowed.
