## Executive judgment

This week emphasizes a **measurement and disciplinary-validity problem**, not a newly established universal AI effect. A November 4 chemistry assessment study found generally acceptable AI-written item construction and psychometric fit, but weak alignment between intended competence levels and empirically observed levels. A music-humanities case study found qualified student acceptance of an AI quiz and resistance where interpretive disciplinary reasoning seemed displaced. Two newly discovered October studies reveal how rarely metacognitive *accuracy* is measured, and how self-reported reliance covaries with perceived load. A conceptual review clarifies the distinction between offloading peripheral versus learning-constitutive cognitive work. None demonstrates long-term deskilling or warrants a Baseline change.

## Verified evidence

### 1. Good Questions, Sound Measurement? Item-Writing Quality, Psychometric Functioning, and Competence-Level Alignment of AI-generated Multiple-Choice Assessments in Chemistry Education

**Marc Rodemer, Julius Franke & David Feldon** · *Frontiers in Education* · **2026-11-04** · **NEW** · **[B]** · **Radars: R2 R4 R5** · **DOI:** [10.3389/feduc.2026.1939585](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1939585) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1939585/abstract)

GPT-4 generated curriculum-anchored chemistry multiple-choice items administered to 95 students. Item-writing criteria were generally met and Rasch fit was acceptable, but language, distractors, option length, and especially alignment between intended competence levels and observed difficulty remained problematic. **Item fit is not evidence that a test measures the intended cognitive competence.**

**Caveat:** One chemistry unit, modest sample and assessment-specific task; does not measure student learning gains or generalize to high-stakes exams.

### 2. Disciplinary Thinking and AI Perception: A Case Study of Undergraduate Students' Evaluation of an AI Music Quiz

**Gavin S. K. Lee, Laura Case, Jeremy Rose, Jocelyn Ho & Andy Tran** · *Frontiers in Education* · **2026-11-04** · **NEW** · **[C]** · **Radars: R3 R4 R6** · **DOI:** [10.3389/feduc.2026.1968048](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1968048) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1968048/abstract)

Thirty-four post-activity surveys in a first-year musicology course found positive perceived understanding but divided views of a gamified adaptive AI quiz. Qualitative responses challenged the fit between right/wrong quiz logic and interpretive, dialogic humanities learning. **Task design must preserve discipline-specific forms of judgment, not only answer accuracy.**

**Caveat:** Small, single-course descriptive case without randomized comparison, independent learning assessment, or longer follow-up.

### 3. Metacognition in AI-Supported Second Language Learning: A Systematic Review of Constructs, Measures, and Evidence (2016–2026)

**Hong Yi, Qiang Chen & Zhuo Wang** · *Journal of Intelligence* · **2026-10-01** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R3 R4** · **DOI:** [10.3390/jintelligence14100236](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence14100236) · [Primary record](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/14/10/236)

Across 60 eligible studies, 56 were post-ChatGPT; 98% of these measured self-reported metacognitive regulation and 66% used self-report alone. **Only one study in the full corpus measured metacognitive accuracy.** Five post-ChatGPT studies paired an AI-absent comparison with process data. The field often measures perceived regulation, not whether users know when they are right.

**Caveat:** L2-specific, predominantly higher education and writing; heterogeneous measures precluded meta-analysis. This documents measurement gaps, not a pooled causal impact.

### 4. University students’ overreliance on generative AI feedback in the learning process: the effects of perceived cognitive load, cognitive offloading, and metacognitive monitoring

**Yuanyuan Guo & Peng Dong** · *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-28** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[B]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R6** · **DOI:** [10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1998976](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1998976) · [Primary record](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1998976/full)

A 600-student cross-sectional survey associated perceived cognitive load with cognitive offloading and self-reported AI-feedback overreliance. Relationships varied descriptively by metacognitive-monitoring group; monitoring was grouped using a single item. **Structural-equation paths do not establish that offloading causes dependence.**

**Caveat:** One university, self-report, cross-sectional design, common-method concerns, and no behavioral or longitudinal validation.

### 5. Augmentation or Erosion? Regulating Cognitive Offloading in Generative AI-Supported STEM Learning

**Daiki Nakamura** · *Educational Psychology Review* · **2026-10-01** · **NEWLY_DISCOVERED** · **[C]** · **Radars: R1 R2 R4** · **DOI:** [10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6) · [Primary record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6)

This conceptual review argues that the relevant design variable is which cognitive operations are delegated: peripheral work can be offloaded without removing the reasoning constitutive of the learning goal. It specifies testable propositions about partition discernment, monitoring, verification and re-internalization.

**Caveat:** The article generated no new participant data. Its propositions are a framework to test, not empirical confirmation of a universal mechanism.
