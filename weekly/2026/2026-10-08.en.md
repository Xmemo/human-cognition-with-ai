## 1｜NEW — In professional practice, *which layer* is delegated matters more than total AI time

### The layer of lesson preparation delegated to AI predicts classroom discourse quality: a four-wave video study of 90 junior secondary mathematics teachers

**Authors:** Hongyu Chen, Na Wang, Chong Chen, Dirong Zhang, Xin Dang  
**Venue:** *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-06**  
**Evidence:** **[B] promising / bounded evidence** · **Radars:** R1 R2 R4  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1962005

Ninety working mathematics teachers from 18 public schools were followed across four waves over one school year, covering 897 video-coded lessons, 1,518 preparation sessions, 4,640 unplanned student contributions, and 556 stimulated-recall interviews.

The study distinguished two forms of AI delegation by tracing lesson-plan provenance against interaction logs. **Generative-layer offloading** meant handing over questioning and variation generation; **formulation-layer offloading** meant handing over context and wording. Within teachers, more generative-layer offloading was associated with less higher-order questioning, less substantive uptake of student talk, weaker maintenance of cognitive demand, and weaker recognition/use of unplanned contributions. Formulation-layer offloading showed a different pattern and was not associated with four of those negative indicators.

An embedded micro-randomized comparison required teachers to draft their own questioning and variation sequence before consulting the model. That human-first sequence reduced generative-layer offloading and increased substantive uptake of student contributions. Teachers' post-preparation confidence also rose with generative-layer offloading without predicting better subsequent classroom performance.

**Why it matters:** This moves the field beyond the coarse variable “AI use.” The operational question becomes **which cognitive operation is delegated, at what stage, and whether human generation precedes model assistance**.

**Caveat:** Most of the school-year evidence is still observational within teachers. The micro-randomized component supports a narrower intervention claim, not a general causal claim that generative offloading degrades expertise. There is also no AI-withdrawal test of durable teacher capability.

## 2｜NEW — Supportive and substitutive AI use correlate with different cognitive profiles, but causality remains open

### Artificial intelligence use, creative thinking disposition, and metacognitive awareness among EFL learners: a structural equation modelling approach

**Authors:** Mohammad Hamad Al-khresheh, Mayez Almayez  
**Venue:** *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* · **2026-10-03**  
**Evidence:** **[B] promising / bounded evidence** · **Radars:** R1 R2 R3 R4  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-09291-5

A cross-sectional study of 713 undergraduate EFL learners separated AI use into cognitive-support, creative-extension, reproductive/substitutive, and ethical/critical-engagement patterns. Cognitive-support and creative-extension use were positively associated with creative-thinking disposition and metacognitive awareness, while reproductive/substitutive use was negatively associated with both outcomes. Ethical/critical engagement moderated some of those associations.

**Why it matters:** It independently reinforces a recurring signal: **use architecture contains more information than raw use frequency**. It also places metacognitive awareness and creative disposition in the same differentiated-use model.

**Caveat:** All focal variables were measured cross-sectionally. The study cannot establish whether use patterns cause cognitive differences, whether pre-existing learner traits shape how AI is used, or whether both are driven by other variables.

## 3｜NEW — Creative support can feel useful without producing corresponding assessed performance gains

### Generative AI in creative education: baseline-linked evidence on learning processes, artistic performance, and authorship

**Authors:** Ningtao Shen, Lai Soon Lee, Aneng He  
**Venue:** *Frontiers in Psychology* · **2026-10-02**  
**Evidence:** **[B] promising / bounded evidence** · **Radars:** R3 R4 R6  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1956939

The study linked prior-course performance, final creative-performance assessment, and post-project questionnaires from 133 undergraduate art students. It distinguished **assistive AI use** for ideation, refinement, process management, and technique learning from **content-generative use** that directly contributed to the creative artifact.

AI use was associated with stronger perceived support and perceived skill transfer, but final artistic performance and baseline-to-final change did not show corresponding improvement. Content-generative use was also associated with lower evaluated authorship.

**Why it matters:** This is another process–outcome dissociation. Fluency, support, and the feeling of skill transfer should not be assumed to equal measured capability or visible authorship.

**Caveat:** The design is observational rather than randomized. Propensity adjustment and baseline-linked models reduce some confounding but do not establish causal effects of either AI-use mode.

## 4｜NEW — A new framework formalizes the “cognitive partition” hypothesis, but it is still theory

### Augmentation or Erosion? Regulating Cognitive Offloading in Generative AI-Supported STEM Learning

**Author:** Daiki Nakamura  
**Venue:** *Educational Psychology Review* · **2026-10-01**  
**Evidence:** **[C] conceptual / exploratory** · **Radars:** R1 R2 R4  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10224-6

This integrative review argues that the educational effect of GenAI should be modeled as a property of the **partition of cognitive labor** rather than a property of the tool itself. Offloading may augment learning when it removes operations peripheral to the current learning goal, but may erode acquisition or maintenance when it bypasses operations constitutive of that goal unless the learner later reconstructs or substantively processes the output.

The paper formalizes “offloading regulation” through partition discernment, calibrated monitoring, disciplinary verification, and re-internalization, and states testable propositions rather than presenting new causal data.

**Why it matters:** The framework fits the empirical direction seen in the teacher study, but is useful mainly because it makes the mechanism falsifiable at the level of cognitive operations.

**Caveat:** This is a theory-building review. Convergence with empirical studies should not be mistaken for independent empirical confirmation of the proposed framework.

## 5｜NEW — Structured AI support can improve assessed learning, but the active ingredient is still bundled

### Integrating generative AI into cognitive apprenticeship–based problem-based learning in biochemistry: a quasi-experimental study

**Authors:** Meng Sheng, Yuqing Gong, Weiwu Hu, Yalin Liu  
**Venue:** *BMC Medical Education* · **2026-10-08**  
**Evidence:** **[B] promising / bounded evidence** · **Radars:** R3 R4 R5  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-10504-3

The study compared an AI-supported cognitive-apprenticeship PBL condition with conventional PBL and lecture-based learning in a biochemistry unit. The AI-CAS-PBL group achieved higher post-intervention lipid-metabolism knowledge and case-based clinical-reasoning scores than both comparison groups and reported lower cognitive workload and higher self-efficacy than conventional PBL.

**Why it matters:** This is useful counterevidence to blanket “AI reduces effort, therefore it reduces learning” narratives. Lower workload can coexist with stronger assessed learning when AI is embedded in a structured pedagogical system.

**Caveat:** The design is quasi-experimental, and AI support, instructor modeling, learning activities, and out-of-class support differ together. The study itself explicitly notes that the AI × instructor-scaffolding interaction was not isolated. It therefore supports the *configuration*, not a single active mechanism.

## 6｜NEW — Authorship governance may need proportional disclosure rather than exhaustive surveillance

### Against mandatory prompt disclosure in AI-assisted scholarship

**Authors:** Brian D. Earp, Udo Schüklenk, Julian Savulescu, Sebastian Porsdam Mann  
**Venue:** *AI & Society* · **2026-10-05**  
**Evidence:** **[C] conceptual / normative review** · **Radar:** R6  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03326-w

The authors argue against mandatory full inclusion of every prompt and model output for ordinary AI-assisted scholarly writing. They instead propose proportionate disclosure: detailed prompt/output records when scientific validity or reproducibility depends on the AI-mediated method, lighter declaration for ordinary writing assistance, and continued human authorship attestation and accountability.

**Why it matters:** The paper distinguishes **provenance needed to evaluate the evidence** from surveillance of every cognitive interaction with AI. That boundary is relevant to authorship, ownership, and responsibility as AI becomes embedded in knowledge work.

**Caveat:** This is a normative policy argument, not evidence that one disclosure regime empirically produces better integrity, authorship, or cognitive outcomes.

# Evidence convergence this week

### 1. The unit of analysis is shifting from “AI use” to the allocation of cognitive operations

The strongest empirical result separates generative-layer from formulation-layer delegation; the conceptual review independently formalizes this as a cognitive-partition problem. The important variable is increasingly **what the human still has to generate, evaluate, reconstruct, or notice**.

### 2. Human-first sequencing is becoming a testable design variable

The teacher micro-randomization adds a more specific intervention than generic “reflection” or “guardrails”: **generate the core questioning/variation structure first, then consult the model**. This does not establish a universal rule, but it gives G03 a cleaner mechanism candidate than previous bundled workflows.

### 3. Reduced effort is not itself evidence of cognitive erosion

The BMC study reports lower perceived workload alongside higher assessed knowledge and clinical-reasoning performance. The relevant distinction is therefore not “effort good / ease bad,” but whether removed effort was constitutive of the target capability or peripheral to it.

### 4. Subjective support, confidence, measured performance, and authorship can diverge

Across the teacher and creative-education studies, confidence/support can rise without parallel improvement in later objective performance, and content-generative participation can complicate evaluated authorship. Monitoring only satisfaction or output fluency remains epistemically weak.

# Conclusions not supported

- The teacher study does **not** show that all generative-layer AI assistance causes professional deskilling.
- The human-first sequence does **not** establish a universal optimal workflow across domains.
- The EFL study does **not** establish causal cognitive benefits or harms from any AI-use orientation.
- The creative-education study does **not** prove that GenAI fails to improve creativity in general.
- The biochemistry study does **not** isolate AI as the causal ingredient of the full instructional package.
- Lower perceived cognitive workload does **not** by itself imply either better learning or cognitive erosion.
- The prompt-disclosure paper does **not** empirically establish that proportional disclosure is superior to full disclosure.

It remains unresolved because the target is classroom practice, not delayed independent capability, and other successful configurations remain multi-component.
