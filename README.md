# Human Cognition with AI

**A bilingual research observatory on human cognition in the age of AI.**

[中文](README.zh-CN.md) · [Current Baseline](research/baseline-2026-08-29.en.md) · [Research Synthesis](research/README.md) · [Research History](research/research-history.en.md) · [Research Map](methodology/research-map.md) · [Bibliography](references/master-bibliography.md)

> **How does human thinking change, persist, and evolve as intelligence becomes increasingly external?**

Human Cognition with AI is a public, bilingual research observatory tracking three linked questions: **how AI changes human cognition; how human–AI systems can augment rather than merely substitute for cognition; and how those changes propagate into groups, norms, collective intelligence, and culture.**

The project is evidence-first. It separates immediate task performance from retained capability, distinguishes empirical findings from theoretical interpretation, and treats both cognitive augmentation and cognitive erosion as open empirical questions.

**Updated weekly · Evidence-first · No AI panic · No AI hype**

## What we know so far — Baseline 2026-08-29

The current evidence does **not** support a simple “AI makes us smarter” or “AI makes us dumber” story. The more defensible picture is conditional:

1. **Better AI-assisted output is not the same as stronger retained human capability.** Learning, transfer, and independent performance after AI removal need to be measured separately.
2. **Cognitive offloading is not a single variable.** What is delegated—and whether humans retain control over goals, problem representation, verification, judgment, and stopping conditions—matters more than raw AI-use frequency.
3. **Metacognition and epistemic agency are emerging as central control variables.** Calibration, verification, reflective judgment, and knowing when *not* to delegate increasingly distinguish augmentation from substitution.
4. **Structured, process-oriented support currently looks more promising than unguided end-to-end delegation.** But most evidence is still short-term and education-heavy; strong longitudinal evidence is scarce.
5. **Human + AI is not automatically synergistic.** Meta-analytic evidence shows that collaboration can underperform the better of the human or AI alone, depending on the task and interaction design.
6. **Writing agency and authorship are distributed across process stages.** Planning, prompting, editing, reviewing, monitoring, verification, and responsibility are not interchangeable—and authorship cannot be reduced to a percentage of words typed.
7. **Machine Culture changes the unit of analysis.** AI may improve individual creative performance while reducing between-person diversity; machine-discovered strategies can also enter, spread through, and persist in human populations under specific conditions.

### What remains unresolved

The highest-priority gaps are **post-AI residual capability**, long-term longitudinal effects, expert-vs-novice divergence, real-world knowledge work, collective variance, and behavioral measures of epistemic agency / authorship / responsibility.

## 🔭 Frontier Track: Machine Culture

A central frontier is **Machine Culture**: how machine-generated strategies, language, norms, and behaviors enter human culture; whether humans can understand and preserve machine discoveries; and how AI changes variation, transmission, selection, and accumulation in cultural evolution.

A key empirical anchor is Brinkmann et al. (2026), who showed that machine-discovered problem-solving strategies can be learned, transferred, socially transmitted, and preserved after the machine is removed when they are non-trivial, learnable, and advantageous ([Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76113-2)).

### Researcher watch: Iyad Rahwan & Center for Humans and Machines

Iyad Rahwan and collaborators at the Max Planck Institute for Human Development are a standing priority because their work connects **machine behaviour, machine culture, human–AI groups, cultural evolution, social learning, algorithmic mediation, and AI metacognition**. The weekly scan checks new papers, preprints, talks, research programs, and collaborator work; inclusion is never automatic.

## Why this project exists

Public debate often collapses several questions into one: *Does AI make us smarter or dumber?* The research is better organized around distinct mechanisms:

- **Assisted performance vs. retained capability** — better output while AI is present is not the same as learning or transfer after AI is removed.
- **Offloading vs. augmentation** — externalizing cognition can be adaptive or substitutive depending on which operations and control points remain human-regulated.
- **Individual vs. collective effects** — a locally efficient workflow may still alter variation, norms, and exploration at population scale.
- **Generation vs. judgment** — as generation becomes cheap, calibration, verification, epistemic agency, and responsibility may become more valuable.

These questions connect literatures on cognitive offloading, distributed cognition, metacognition, human–AI collaboration, hybrid intelligence, cognitive resilience, authorship, collective intelligence, and cultural evolution.

## Evidence Policy

Each retained item receives a visible evidence label:

- **[A] Strong empirical evidence** — robust experiments, rigorous systematic reviews/meta-analyses, or convergent evidence.
- **[B] Promising / moderate evidence** — credible empirical work with meaningful limitations, high-quality preprints, or narrower experimental evidence.
- **[C] Conceptual / exploratory** — theory, perspective, qualitative or early-stage work that requires narrower interpretation.
- **[S] Strategic signal** — lab programs, workshops, institutional changes, research agendas, or policy signals; not empirical proof.

Full criteria: [Evidence grading](methodology/evidence-grading.md).

## Weekly Method

Every weekly run:

1. scans **all nine radars**;
2. searches recent papers/preprints **and** research-program/lab/report/event signals;
3. prioritizes primary/canonical sources and verifies bibliographic metadata;
4. builds one candidate pool across all radars;
5. selects up to **12 highest-value items** rather than filling one quota per radar;
6. allows quiet radars to remain empty rather than padding weak material;
7. gives standing search priority to Rahwan / Center for Humans and Machines;
8. freezes one evidence set before producing Chinese and English public editions.

## Bilingual Publishing

The Chinese and English public editions use **one frozen evidence set**. Chinese is the canonical editorial edition; English is rewritten idiomatically from the same sources rather than generated through a second independent research pass. Paper titles, DOI records, evidence grades, caveats, and source identity remain aligned.

## Scope

This repository is a research observatory, not a claim that every included paper is correct. It does not equate citation count with evidence quality, does not infer authorship from AI-detector scores, and does not convert correlational or short-term findings into long-term causal claims.
