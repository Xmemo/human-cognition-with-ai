> **How does human thinking change, persist, and evolve as intelligence becomes increasingly external?**

Human Cognition with AI tracks empirical research and high-signal research programs on how generative AI changes human cognitive processes, how AI systems can augment rather than merely replace cognition, and how these changes propagate through groups and culture. The project is deliberately evidence-first: it separates immediate task performance from retained human capability, distinguishes empirical findings from conceptual interpretation, and treats both augmentation and erosion as open empirical questions.

**Updated weekly · Evidence-first · No AI panic · No AI hype**

## 🔭 Frontier Track: Machine Culture

A central frontier of this observatory is **Machine Culture**: research on how machine-generated strategies, language, norms, and behaviours enter human culture; whether humans can understand and preserve machine discoveries; and how AI changes variation, transmission, selection, and accumulation in cultural evolution.

A key reference is Brinkmann et al. (2026), who experimentally showed that machine-discovered problem-solving strategies can be transmitted, understood, and preserved in human populations when they are non-trivial, learnable, and advantageous ([Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76113-2)).

## Why this project exists

The public debate often collapses several different questions into one: *Does AI make us smarter or dumber?* The research does not support such a one-dimensional framing. More useful distinctions include:

- **Assisted performance vs. retained capability:** better output while AI is present is not the same as learning or transfer after AI is removed.
- **Offloading vs. augmentation:** externalizing cognition can be adaptive, substitutive, or disruptive depending on which cognitive operations and control points remain human-regulated.
- **Individual vs. collective effects:** a workflow that is locally efficient for one user can still alter variation, norms, and exploration at population scale.
- **Generation vs. judgment:** as generation becomes cheap, calibration, verification, epistemic agency, and responsibility may become more important.

These distinctions are grounded in established and emerging literatures on cognitive offloading, distributed cognition, metacognition, human–AI collaboration, hybrid intelligence, cognitive resilience, authorship, and cultural evolution.

## Evidence Policy

Each retained item receives a visible evidence label:

- **[A] Strong empirical evidence** — e.g. well-designed experiments, rigorous systematic reviews/meta-analyses, convergent evidence.
- **[B] Promising / moderate evidence** — useful empirical work with meaningful limitations, high-quality preprints, smaller or context-limited studies.
- **[C] Conceptual / exploratory** — theory, commentary, perspective, qualitative or early-stage work whose claims require narrower interpretation.
- **[S] Strategic signal** — lab programs, policy/institutional changes, workshops, research agendas, or field-level developments; not treated as empirical proof.

Full criteria: [Evidence grading](methodology/evidence-grading.md).

## Weekly Method

Every weekly run:

1. scans **all nine radars**;
2. searches recent papers/preprints **and** research-program/lab/report/event signals;
3. prioritizes primary/canonical sources and verifies bibliographic metadata;
4. builds one candidate pool across all radars;
5. selects the **12 highest-value items** rather than filling one quota per radar;
6. explicitly allows a radar to have no qualifying signal;
7. checks Rahwan / Center for Humans and Machines as a priority frontier watch;
8. freezes one evidence set before producing Chinese and English public editions.

## Bilingual Publishing

The Chinese and English briefs use **one frozen evidence set**. Chinese is the canonical editorial edition; the English edition is rewritten idiomatically from the same sources rather than produced through a second research pass. Paper titles, author names, DOI records, evidence grades, caveats, and source identity remain aligned across languages.
