# Cognitive Ecology: From Cognitive Offloading to Human–AI–World Coupling

**Topic status: exploratory cross-cutting lens (2026-10-09) | Not a new evidence baseline | No tenth radar**

[中文](cognitive-ecology.zh-CN.md) · [Current Baseline](baseline-2026-08-29.en.md) · [3×3 Map](../methodology/research-map.md) · [Cognitive Loop Protocol](../methodology/cognitive-loop-protocol.md)

> Research proposition: impacts of AI on cognition cannot be inferred from output quality or quantity of offloading alone. Study how biological agents, bodies, representations, AI tools, and other people couple into feedback loops, adapt, and sometimes fail.

## 1 | Why this layer?

The existing Control / Retention / Variance model stays intact:
- **Control**: who sets goals, frames problems, checks evidence, judges, and decides to stop?
- **Retention**: what can the individual explain, reconstruct, transfer, or repair unaided?
- **Variance**: what diversity of search remains across people using similar AI systems?

These are **organizing outcome/governance dimensions**, not a full model of how thinking takes place. The body–space–social synthesis in Annie Murphy Paul's *The Extended Mind* (2021) motivates looking beyond a linear input–computation–output account of human activity.

**Cognitive Ecology** is therefore a mechanism-oriented, cross-cutting lens:

1. **Resources / context**: prior knowledge, body and interoception, physical/digital representations, AI systems, social relationships.
2. **Coupling / loops**: expressing, manipulating, checking, conversing, acting in the world, receiving feedback, revising.
3. **Outcomes**: Control / Retention / Variance plus performance, transfer, resilience, experience, and cost.

It is **not a validated single theory, general-purpose psychometric measure, or claim of product efficacy**.

## 2 | Foundations and evidence limits

| Reference | Contribution | Evidence / limit |
|---|---|---|
| Clark & Chalmers (1998), [The Extended Mind](https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/58.1.7) | Philosophical account of coupled external representations | [C] Philosophy, not evidence that every tool enhances cognition |
| Hutchins (1995), [Cognition in the Wild](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/1881.001.0001) | Cognition distributed across navigators, artifacts, and social organization | [C] Theory / ethnography, not an AI intervention trial |
| Kirsh & Maglio (1994), [Epistemic vs Pragmatic Action](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15516709COG1804_1) | Actions can simplify a cognitive problem by revealing otherwise hidden information | [B] Behavioral data in specific tasks, not universal AI UI effects |
| Oppezzo & Schwartz (2014), [Give Your Ideas Some Legs](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0036577) | Walking supports divergent ideation in four experiments | [B] Task-specific; not a general decision-making benefit |
| Goldin-Meadow, Cook & Mitchell (2009), [Gesturing Gives Children New Ideas About Math](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02297.x) | Gesture manipulation affects children's learning in a specific math setting | [B] Population- and task-specific |
| Zhao & Han (2026), [Understanding human–AI interaction: distributed cognition](https://doi.org/10.1108/JD-01-2026-0003) | Five-phase interpretive loop: intent, externalization, generation, assessment, update | [C] Conceptual synthesis, not proof of effectiveness |
| Cassinadri & Bianchini (2026), [Varieties and dynamics of cognitive extensions via AI systems](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogsys.2026.101507) | Constitutive, complementary, and substitutive AI–agent relationships; extension need not enhance | [C] Conceptual mini-review, not an intervention comparison |
| Paul (2021), [The Extended Mind: The Power of Thinking Outside the Brain](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780544947665) | Accessible synthesis on bodily, spatial, and social resources | [C] Secondary science writing, not a substitute for primary evidence |

**Keep concepts distinct:** the extended-mind thesis addresses the boundaries of cognition; distributed cognition analyzes activity systems; embodied cognition emphasizes bodily contributions. They overlap but are not equivalent.

## 3 | Retention is necessary but not sufficient

The existing baseline rightly insists on *AI-off* testing to separate impressive assisted output from actual learning. That requirement should not be discarded.

Yet removing paper, diagrams, navigation tools, or AI can lower immediate task performance even when the tool has expanded what the coupled system can accomplish. This alone does **not** establish loss of underlying cognitive capacity. Add three distinct outcome families under Retention without prematurely renaming the top-level dimensions:

1. **Internal retention** — unaided explanation, recall, reconstruction, near/far transfer.
2. **Extended capability** — what the human plus external system can now do; compare human-only and AI-only conditions.
3. **System resilience** — detect errors and maintain/recover performance under AI failure, misleading output, or tool replacement.

Record task quality, time/effort, calibration, and ownership independently. Do not equate dependence on a tool with loss of agency or deskilling.

## 4 | A falsifiable interaction hypothesis

> For tasks requiring learning or judgment, preserving an iterative sequence of initial human representation → external action / conversation → evidence comparison → human evaluation → revision **may** support better independent transfer, calibration, or ownership than immediate end-to-end answers; it can also cost time and fail on simple tasks.

Caveats:
- A loop is not a mandatory ritual; allow skipping, stopping, and justified delegation.
- Body movement, drawing, and human conversation are optional resources, not universal prescriptions.
- A reminder to take a break is not itself evidence of embodied cognitive augmentation.
- AI agents can also perform loops: assess **which critical feedback nodes include or exclude the human**.

For measurement details, see the [Cognitive Loop Protocol](../methodology/cognitive-loop-protocol.md).

## 6 | Product hypotheses — candidate Research Lab experiments only

| Candidate | Minimal intervention | Primary test | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thinking Loop | Initial view → AI challenge → evidence check → revised view | Delayed independent performance / calibration | More turns without better understanding |
| Walk & Think | Optional walk/voice capture before revising a problem | Divergent ideation quality and diversity | Effort added with no incremental benefit |
| Spatial Thinking | Rearrange concepts, then ask AI to critique relations | Explanations and error detection | Attractive diagrams, no improved reasoning |
| Social Thinking | Take claims into genuine conversations and collect contrary evidence | Evidence-linked belief revision | Simulated discussion crowds out social contact |
| Soul Agent × Kapi | Voluntary embodied pause before reconsidering action | Agency, state, subsequent behavior | Physiological signals mistaken for correctness |

**Observatory / lab firewall:** product concepts are hypotheses, not privileged evidence. Experimental promotion requires explicit human choice, suitable controls, and retention of null results.

## 7 | Claims not supported

- AI use necessarily causes cognitive decline.
- Embodied interaction necessarily beats screen-only thinking.
- All cognitive offloading should be prevented.
- Extended-mind philosophy establishes an AI app's effectiveness.
- AI-off performance drops *by themselves* prove deskilling.
- Cognitive loops universally improve mental health, creativity, or judgment.

Update the canonical baseline only when replicated, task-relevant comparisons and real AI-on/off/transfer or longitudinal evidence justify a change.
