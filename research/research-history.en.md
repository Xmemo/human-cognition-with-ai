# Research History & Migration

[中文](research-history.zh-CN.md) · [Current Baseline](baseline-2026-08-29.en.md)

**Human Cognition with AI** did not begin on 2026-08-28. It consolidates several earlier research threads on cognitive protection/augmentation, AI-assisted writing as a mode of thinking, and cognitive engineering.

This migration follows a strict rule:

> **Stable questions, conceptual structures and working hypotheses may migrate; old papers, numbers or strong causal claims that cannot be re-verified do not automatically enter the current evidence baseline.**

This page is therefore an intellectual history of the project, not a scientific evidence list.

---

## Phase 0｜From “Does AI make us dumber?” to cognitive redistribution

The earliest shift was away from the coarse question of whether AI makes people smarter or dumber.

A more useful starting point was **cognitive redistribution / cognitive offloading**: when search, generation, memory, summarization, reasoning and even evaluation can be delegated to external intelligence, cognitive work is not merely reduced—it is redistributed.

Three durable questions emerged:

1. **Which cognitive work is worth offloading?**
2. **Which operations affect skill formation, judgment or autonomy when delegated?**
3. **How does a person know when to think internally and when to call AI?**

The third question naturally moved the project toward metacognition.

---

## Phase 1｜The “Cognitive Protection & Augmentation” trilogy

An earlier three-part research architecture emerged:

### Part 1｜Cognitive redistribution and metacognitive scheduling

The working hypothesis was that **metacognition is not merely another capability parallel to critical thinking; it acts more like a scheduling/control layer.**

It monitors:

```text
What problem am I solving?
→ What do I already know?
→ Which part should be delegated?
→ How trustworthy is the AI output?
→ Do I need verification / challenge / further search?
→ When should I stop?
```

Critical thinking, retrieval, comparison and verification are then operations that this control layer can invoke.

### Part 2｜Four high-value / high-risk contexts

The earlier map emphasized:

- education and learning;
- writing;
- creativity;
- expert cognition.

These are no longer maintained as four peer-level domains. Experts and writers are better treated as cross-cutting contexts rather than mechanisms at the same level as cognitive change or augmentation.

### Part 3｜Cognitive engineering

The project then moved from diagnosis toward design:

> If AI can complete more and more cognitive work, how should interfaces and workflows be designed so AI becomes a **tool for thought** rather than merely an answer machine?

This line directly evolved into the current **Cognitive Augmentation & Governance** domain.

---

## Phase 2｜AI + Writing + Thinking

This phase sharpened a specific research question:

> **In AI-native writing environments, how can writing remain a mechanism for forming human thought rather than becoming hollowed out by AI ghostwriting?**

The analysis shifted from “Was the text written by AI?” to the writing process:

- planning;
- idea generation;
- translating / drafting;
- reviewing;
- monitoring;
- revision;
- evidence checking.

Several durable working hypotheses emerged.

### 1. Writing-as-thinking

The value of writing is not only the final artifact. Writing externalizes thought, forces organization, surfaces contradictions and changes the writer’s internal model.

### 2. Necessary cognitive operations

The goal is not to make writing artificially difficult. It is to preserve operations that themselves generate learning and thought: forming a first judgment, comparing evidence, reconstructing an argument, noticing dissatisfaction, and revising.

### 3. Process ownership

Authorship should not be inferred only from how many words a human typed. Problem framing, core claims, structure, selection, rejection, verification and responsibility are separate control points.

By 2025–2026, HCI and writing research began providing more direct empirical anchors for these questions, so they now live inside R3 and R6 rather than remaining purely internal hypotheses.

---

## Phase 3｜From Cognitive Protection to Human Cognition with AI

A naming problem became increasingly clear.

**Cognitive Protection** presupposes a defensive stance.

But the evidence space includes:

- erosion / dependence;
- augmentation;
- hybrid intelligence;
- machine-discovered knowledge;
- collective intelligence;
- Machine Culture.

The project therefore became:

# Human Cognition with AI

The research object comes before the value judgment.

The central question became:

> **As intelligence becomes increasingly external, how does human cognition change, how can it be augmented, and how do those changes propagate into groups and cultural evolution?**

---

## Phase 4｜From individual cognition to Machine Culture

Machine Culture introduced the largest scope expansion.

The first two layers mainly study:

```text
Human
↕
AI
```

The third studies:

```text
Humans ↔ AI ↔ Networks ↔ Norms ↔ Culture
```

Earlier individual-level questions then acquire population-level analogues:

| Individual level | Population / cultural level |
|---|---|
| cognitive offloading | cultural outsourcing / machine mediation |
| loss of writer voice | population-level convergence |
| learning & transfer | cultural transmission & retention |
| individual exploration | collective variance |
| AI dependence | cultural dependence |
| cognitive augmentation | cumulative cultural augmentation |

This table is a research bridge, not a claim that every term in the right column is already an established construct.

# Why the project converged on a 3 × 3 radar

The current map is:

```text
01 | HUMAN COGNITIVE CHANGE
   ├── Offloading, Skill Formation & Expertise
   ├── Metacognition, Epistemic Agency & Judgment
   └── Critical Thinking, Memory, Creativity & Writing Cognition

02 | COGNITIVE AUGMENTATION & GOVERNANCE
   ├── Tools for Thought & Cognitive Augmentation
   ├── Human–AI Collaboration & Hybrid Intelligence
   └── Cognitive Resilience, Authorship & Responsibility

03 | MACHINE CULTURE & COLLECTIVE COGNITION
   ├── Machine Culture & Cultural Evolution
   ├── Collective Intelligence & Human–AI Groups
   └── Social Learning, Norms & Cultural Feedback
```

The 3×3 design uses three criteria:

1. **Searchable** — each radar can support stable academic queries.
2. **Discriminative** — radars should capture distinct mechanisms rather than return the same education papers repeatedly.
3. **Actionable** — a new finding should clearly indicate which part of the baseline it updates.

Experts, writers, clinicians, programmers and researchers are treated as **cross-cutting context tags** rather than top-level taxonomy branches.

# Migration audit: what was preserved and what was not

## Preserved

- cognitive redistribution rather than simple “more/less thinking”;
- metacognition as a scheduling/control-layer working model;
- the distinction between performance gain and learning/transfer;
- writing-as-thinking and process ownership;
- Tools for Thought / cognitive engineering;
- sustained interest in expertise, agency and responsibility;
- expansion from individual cognition to collective cognition / Machine Culture.

## Not promoted directly into fact

- old numerical claims whose original source could not be re-located;
- “latest research” items from old briefs whose publication dates were not freshly verified;
- interpretations of short-term experiments as long-term deskilling;
- internal concepts such as “necessary struggle,” “cognitive ownership,” or “cognitive reserve” where no mature academic construct yet exists;
- strong causal claims without explicit evidence grading.

These can still act as hypothesis generators, but they must re-enter through the current nine-radar evidence process.

# The current research loop

```text
Legacy questions / hypotheses
          ↓
3×3 Radar Search
          ↓
Verified evidence pool
          ↓
Weekly Briefs
          ↓
Evidence convergence / contradiction
          ↓
Baseline update
          ↓
Research map revision (only when necessary)
```

The repository is therefore not a dump of old conversations. It is a way to preserve the strongest questions from earlier work inside a **public research system that can be corrected by new evidence over time**.
