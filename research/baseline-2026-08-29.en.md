## 0. What this baseline is

Weekly briefs answer “what changed recently?” This baseline asks a more stable question: **Which claims are currently supported well enough to serve as the starting point for the next round of research?**

This refresh follows three constraints:

1. Re-scan the **3 domains × 9 radars** rather than copying an older chat bibliography.
2. Prefer DOI/publisher/conference/institutional records for scientific facts; any Consensus-discovered paper retained in the project is individually fetched before entering the traceable ledger.
3. Separate **empirical findings, theoretical interpretation, strategic signals, and working hypotheses**.

This is a living baseline, not a systematic review of the entire literature.

# 1. Evidence convergence: eight current claims

| Current claim | Evidence position | Defensible wording |
|---|---|---|
| Better AI-assisted performance ≠ stronger human capability | **A/B** | Assisted performance must be separated from learning, transfer and independent performance after AI removal. |
| Cognitive offloading depends on what is offloaded and where control remains | **B** | Offloading is not inherently harmful; replacing core reasoning/evaluation/self-regulation differs from using AI as a scaffold. |
| Metacognition / epistemic agency is a central control layer | **B/C** | Calibration, verification, monitoring and decisions about when not to delegate increasingly explain variation in outcomes. |
| Structured/scaffolded use is generally more promising than unguided substitution | **A/B** | Multiple reviews and experiments point this way, but long-term transfer evidence remains thin. |
| Human + AI does not automatically create synergy | **A** | Task type, baseline capability, interaction design and division of labor determine whether collaboration helps. |
| Writing ownership/authorship is distributed across process stages | **B/C** | Planning, prompting, editing, reviewing and monitoring contribute differently to agency and ownership. |
| AI may raise average creative performance while compressing between-person diversity | **B** | This is a key bridge from individual augmentation to population-level Machine Culture. |
| Machine Culture has moved from conceptual agenda to experimental science | **B/C** | Machine discoveries can enter, spread and persist in human groups under specific conditions; long-term societal effects remain unknown. |

A/B/C here describes how strongly a claim can currently be stated, not a simple quality ranking of individual papers.

# 2. Domain 1｜Human Cognitive Change

## R1｜Cognitive Offloading, Skill Formation & Expertise

### 2.1 Lower cognitive load cannot automatically be interpreted as a benefit

A systematic review published on 27 August 2026 included 39 empirical studies in educational settings. Its most important result is methodological: studies measure different kinds of load, germane load is rarely measured directly, and a lower overall load can mean either removal of wasteful friction or displacement of cognitive processing required for learning. The authors therefore combine Cognitive Load Theory with cognitive offloading. [Qian et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1921504)

A core measurement rule follows:

> **Do not ask only how much easier AI makes a task; ask which cognitive operation became easier or disappeared.**

### 2.2 Offloading is beginning to split into autonomous vs. dependent forms

Zhu et al. (2026) distinguish **dependent cognitive offloading**—delegating core thinking to AI—from **autonomous cognitive offloading**, where AI scaffolds the task while the user retains cognitive agency. [Zhu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1878629)

This is more informative than raw AI-use duration or prompt counts, but the evidence remains heavily education-based and does not establish long-term deskilling.

### 2.3 “Conditional” is still the best description of the literature

Alubthane (2026) reviewed 89 peer-reviewed higher-education studies from 2024–2026: 40.4% reported positive outcomes and 23.6% mixed/conditional outcomes; over-reliance, reduced analytical autonomy and cognitive offloading were recurring risks. The review proposes an amplifier-vs-substitute dual mechanism. [Alubthane, 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1863931)

A 123-study scoping review similarly organized findings into agency-supportive and agency-eroding patterns and emphasized that heterogeneous evidence should not be interpreted as a single causal effect. [Wang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071150)

### Current position

- **Supported:** AI can reduce low-value burden and improve immediate performance.
- **Supported:** different forms of offloading should be studied separately.
- **Not yet supported:** frequent GenAI use generally causes long-term skill decline. Longitudinal and post-AI transfer evidence is still required.

## R2｜Metacognition, Epistemic Agency & Judgment

### 2.4 Metacognition is becoming a control problem for human–AI systems

Measurement work on collaborative AI metacognition asks whether users know when to accept, challenge, verify or reject AI output. Sidra and Mason’s scale-development study found collaborative AI metacognition explained variance beyond general metacognition. [Sidra & Mason, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2025.2543997)

A 2026 theoretical framework uses **epistemic co-agency** to describe knowledge construction with AI while preserving the learner’s capacity to reason “with, through, and against” generative systems. [Learning with machines, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100573)

On the machine side, Rahwan and collaborators argue for AI metacognition in “wise machines”: intellectual humility, perspective-taking and context adaptability as strategies for managing object-level reasoning. [Johnson et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.002)

### Current position

This project currently treats metacognition as a **scheduling/control layer**:

```text
Goal & problem representation
→ Decide whether to call AI
→ Monitor output
→ Calibrate trust
→ Verify / challenge
→ Accept, revise, search further, or stop
```

This remains a working model; operational definitions of epistemic agency are not yet standardized across fields.

## R3｜Critical Thinking, Memory, Creativity & Writing Cognition

### 2.5 Critical thinking evidence is not a simple “AI helps” vs. “AI harms” result

Helal et al. (2025) synthesized 68 peer-reviewed papers and proposed a dual-impact framework in which prompt quality, self-regulation, engagement, trust and metacognitive critique can shift GenAI toward enhancement or erosion. [Helal et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1108/IDD-05-2025-0125)

Raitskaya and Tikhonova (2025) reviewed 30 empirical studies and likewise found more favorable outcomes when instructional design explicitly preserved active reasoning, reflection and metacognitive monitoring. [Raitskaya & Tikhonova, 2025](https://doi.org/10.17323/jle.2025.27387)

### 2.6 Creativity shows an important mean–variance split

A meta-analysis by Holzner, Maier and Feuerriegel pooled 28 studies with 8,214 participants. Human+GenAI improved average creative performance relative to human-only work (Hedges’ g = 0.27), while idea diversity declined substantially (g = -0.86). [Holzner et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.17241)

The field therefore needs to measure not only:

> “Did each individual become more creative?”

but also:

> **“Did a population become more similar?”**

That directly connects R3 to R7 Machine Culture.

### 2.7 Writing research is shifting from output quality to process ownership

Reza et al. (2025) reviewed 109 HCI papers and interviewed 15 writers, mapping AI writing support across planning, translating, reviewing and monitoring. Desired ownership varied by stage and writing context. [Reza et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3757566)

A preregistered experiment by Pennanen et al. (2026; N=273) found that direct editing increased psychological ownership; iterative prompting increased ownership when editing was unavailable; fully manual writing still produced higher ownership than every AI-assisted condition. [Pennanen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2026.100381)

### Current position

Authorship should not be reduced to:

> human words / total words

A more useful research target is who framed the problem, formed the core claim, chose the structure, verified evidence, rejected suggestions, and can explain and take responsibility for the result. This is a theoretical synthesis, not yet a validated unified scale.

# 3. Domain 2｜Cognitive Augmentation & Governance

## R4｜Tools for Thought & Cognitive Augmentation

### 3.1 The field is moving from “protect cognition” to implementable tools

The Microsoft Research / CHI Tools for Thought community used 2025 largely to map the field; the CHI 2026 workshop explicitly shifted toward **operational frameworks, principles, and tools** that provoke critical thinking, support tutoring and enable sensemaking rather than merely complete tasks. [CHI 2026 Tools for Thought](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/tools-for-thought-understanding-protecting-and-augmenting-human-cognition-with-generative-ai-from-vision-to-implementation/)

This supports a core design distinction:

```text
End-to-end answer delivery
≠
Process-oriented cognitive support
```

Promising variables include prior human prediction/model formation, staged hints, uncertainty exposure, evidence comparison, AI-free retrieval/reconstruction, and explanation/challenge/repair before final submission. These are research directions—not universally validated product recipes.

---

## R5｜Human–AI Collaboration & Hybrid Intelligence

### 3.2 Human + AI is not automatically stronger

Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone’s preregistered systematic review/meta-analysis covered 106 experiments and 370 effect sizes. On average, human–AI combinations performed below the better of human or AI alone; gains were more common in content-creation tasks and losses more common in decision tasks. [Vaccaro et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1)

A 2026 clinical-medicine meta-analysis found improvement in some composite diagnostic/management scores, but prediction intervals were wide, leaving substantial real-world uncertainty. [Wang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02382-2)

**Hybrid intelligence should therefore be measured, not assumed.** At minimum:

```text
Human alone
AI alone
Human + AI
Human after AI is removed
```

The final condition is still especially under-studied.

## R6｜Cognitive Resilience, Authorship & Responsibility

### 3.3 Cognitive resilience is a useful design frame, but much of it remains conceptual

Westerbeek (2026), drawing on distributed cognition, argues that GenAI changes the price structure of cognitive effort: tasks that previously required attention, retrieval, reasoning and judgment can now bypass those operations. [Westerbeek, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03284-3)

The design target is therefore not “effort” itself, but **cognitive operations with developmental value**.

Unceta et al. (2026) introduce **epistemic debt**: the gap created when a user can produce/use/publish an output whose underlying reasoning they cannot adequately understand, verify or defend. [Unceta et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01294-w)

It is a useful explanatory concept, but should currently be treated as a **C-level conceptual construct**, not a mature validated metric with established dose-response relationships.

# 4. Domain 3｜Machine Culture & Collective Cognition

## R7｜Machine Culture & Cultural Evolution

### 4.1 The unit of analysis expands from human–machine interaction to cultural evolution

Rahwan and colleagues’ 2023 *Nature Human Behaviour* perspective framed Machine Culture around machine effects on cultural **variation, transmission and selection**. [Machine Culture, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01742-2)

In 2026 Brinkmann et al. tested cultural transmission directly. Machines discovered a counterintuitive strategy that humans almost never found independently. When a machine discovery was non-trivial, learnable and clearly advantageous, humans learned, transferred, articulated and preserved it across later generations after machines left. Nine of 15 human–machine populations preserved the optimal strategy through all subsequent generations; 14 of 15 human-only populations never discovered it. [Brinkmann et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76113-2)

A stricter loop for machine-to-human cultural augmentation is therefore:

```text
Machine discovery
→ Human learning
→ Human understanding / transfer
→ Social transmission
→ Persistence after machine removal
```

---

## R8｜Collective Intelligence & Human–AI Groups

### 4.2 Individual optimum and long-run group optimum may conflict

Zhong, Eisenmann, Garcia and Rahwan (2026) use agent-based modeling and evolutionary game theory to compare AI-complement and AI-substitute use. In their model, substitute strategies dominate under individual-level selection while reducing cultural variance more strongly; under sufficiently strong group boundaries, complement groups can benefit from maintaining variance for exploration. [Zhong et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03541)

This is a **model, not evidence that cultural collapse is already occurring**. Its value is to formulate a testable collective-action problem:

> Could individually efficient AI use systematically reduce a population’s exploratory variance?

Research on collective intelligence in pure LLM-agent groups is also growing quickly, but machine-only multi-agent results should not be treated as direct evidence about human–AI collective cognition.

---

## R9｜Social Learning, Norms & Cultural Feedback

### 4.3 AI can already alter both what people encounter and potentially how they speak

Baumann et al. (2026) used a sock-puppet audit of TikTok and found rapid amplification of interest-aligned content, typically within the first ~200 videos; stronger amplification was negatively associated with hashtag diversity. [Baumann et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-026-00629-2)

Yakura et al. analyzed roughly 280,000 English presentation/talk/speech videos across more than 20,000 YouTube channels and reported post-ChatGPT shifts in words disproportionately associated with LLM output, providing evidence consistent with LLM influence on spoken language. [Yakura et al.](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.01754)

This motivates a long-run feedback loop:

```text
Human culture
→ training data / algorithmic model
→ machine output / recommendation
→ human learning & behavior
→ changed culture
→ future models
```

Current evidence supports measurable feedback signals, not a claim of inevitable or irreversible cultural homogenization.

# 5. A compact working model

As of this baseline, the project organizes AI-related cognitive change along three measurement dimensions:

## A. Control｜Who controls critical cognitive nodes?

Who controls goals, problem representation, generation, verification, evaluation, stopping conditions and responsibility?

## B. Retention｜What remains after AI leaves?

Can the user explain, reconstruct, transfer, repair and extend the capability?

## C. Variance｜How much collective search space remains?

When many people share similar models and recommendation mechanisms, can individual performance rise while between-person diversity and exploration shrink?

These dimensions connect to:

```text
Control   → Metacognition / Epistemic Agency
Retention → Learning / Skill Formation / Cognitive Resilience
Variance  → Collective Cognition / Machine Culture
```

**This is a research-organizing model, not a single empirically validated grand theory.**

# 6. Evidence needed to upgrade the next baseline

Priority order:

1. Longitudinal studies of months or years of real-world use.
2. Within-person AI-on / AI-off / transfer designs.
3. Expert-vs-novice comparisons in real knowledge work.
4. Measures that separate removal of extraneous load from removal of productive processing.
5. Replicable behavioral measures of agency, authorship and epistemic responsibility.
6. Longitudinal population-level data on creative variance, language, norms and social learning.
7. Direct comparisons of human–AI groups with human-only and AI-only groups.
