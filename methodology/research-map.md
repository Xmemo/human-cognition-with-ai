# Research Map｜3 Domains × 9 Radars

This map is optimized for **weekly discovery**, not for producing a perfectly exhaustive academic taxonomy. A useful radar must be searchable, discriminative, and actionable. Papers can carry multiple radar tags.

## Domain 1 — Human Cognitive Change

### R1. Cognitive Offloading, Skill Formation & Expertise
**Core question:** When AI performs cognitive work, what happens to retained human capability, learning, transfer, and expertise?

**Canonical searches:**
- `cognitive offloading generative AI`
- `AI dependence independent performance`
- `skill formation generative AI`
- `deskilling reskilling AI`
- `expert novice generative AI`

**Typical evidence:** randomized or quasi-experiments, longitudinal studies, learning-transfer tests, expert–novice comparisons, systematic reviews.

**Include when:** the study measures human capability, learning, transfer, skill retention, expertise gaps, or depth of delegation.

**Noise / exclude:** productivity-only studies with no human cognitive outcome; opinion pieces that infer deskilling without evidence.

### R2. Metacognition, Epistemic Agency & Judgment
**Core question:** Do people know what they know, when AI is reliable, and when judgment should remain human?

**Canonical searches:**
- `metacognition generative AI`
- `AI metacognitive calibration`
- `epistemic agency AI`
- `AI overreliance calibration`
- `automation bias generative AI`
- `cognitive autonomy AI`

**Typical evidence:** calibration studies, confidence/accuracy experiments, judgment-of-learning research, decision-support experiments, metacognition scales, epistemic-agency studies.

**Include when:** a study measures monitoring, confidence calibration, verification, judgment, reliance, autonomy, or epistemic control.

**Noise / exclude:** generic AI-literacy surveys without a cognitive or judgment mechanism.

### R3. Critical Thinking, Memory, Creativity & Writing Cognition
**Core question:** Which higher-order cognitive outcomes change when humans use generative AI?

**Canonical searches:**
- `generative AI critical thinking`
- `generative AI memory`
- `AI creativity human`
- `AI cognitive effort`
- `AI writing cognition`
- `generative AI reasoning human`

**Typical evidence:** controlled experiments, memory/retrieval tests, creativity measures, writing-process studies, cognitive-effort measures, longitudinal literacy studies.

**Include when:** outcomes concern critical reasoning, memory, creativity, writing cognition, cognitive effort, or process quality.

**Noise / exclude:** text-quality evaluation without evidence about the human process.

## Domain 2 — Cognitive Augmentation & Governance

### R4. Tools for Thought & Cognitive Augmentation
**Core question:** How should AI support the process of thinking so that humans build or retain capability rather than only receive end-to-end answers?

**Canonical searches:**
- `tools for thought generative AI`
- `cognitive augmentation AI`
- `process-oriented AI support`
- `AI scaffolding cognition`
- `Socratic AI learning`
- `reflection prompt AI cognition`

**Typical evidence:** HCI experiments, interface studies, educational interventions, process-oriented vs. answer-oriented comparisons, workshop syntheses.

**Include when:** the intervention explicitly changes the cognitive workflow or interaction design.

**Noise / exclude:** prompt-engineering tips evaluated only by output quality.

### R5. Human–AI Collaboration & Hybrid Intelligence
**Core question:** Under what conditions does human + AI outperform, complement, or undermine either party alone?

**Canonical searches:**
- `human AI collaboration`
- `human AI complementarity`
- `hybrid intelligence`
- `human AI teaming cognition`
- `human AI symbiosis`
- `human generative AI collaboration performance`

**Typical evidence:** human-only vs. AI-only vs. combined experiments, meta-analyses, hybrid-intelligence systems, professional collaboration studies.

**Include when:** comparison makes complementarity, task allocation, collaboration, or synergy testable.

**Noise / exclude:** marketing claims that any AI-assisted workflow is automatically “hybrid intelligence.”

### R6. Cognitive Resilience, Authorship & Responsibility
**Core question:** How can long-term AI use preserve cognitive agency, authorship, accountability, and the ability to explain or defend outputs?

**Canonical searches:**
- `cognitive resilience generative AI`
- `cognitive sustainability AI`
- `AI authorship agency`
- `AI writing psychological ownership`
- `epistemic responsibility AI`
- `human oversight generative AI`

**Typical evidence:** cognitive-resilience frameworks, authorship/ownership experiments, institutional policies, responsibility frameworks, intervention research.

**Include when:** work addresses durable capability, authorship, ownership, explainability-to-self, responsibility, or non-delegable judgment.

**Noise / exclude:** plagiarism-detection articles that reduce authorship to whether text “looks AI-generated.”

## Domain 3 — Machine Culture & Collective Cognition

### R7. Machine Culture & Cultural Evolution
**Core question:** Can machines become sources of cultural variation that humans learn, preserve, transmit, and build on?

**Canonical searches:**
- `machine culture AI`
- `AI cultural evolution`
- `cumulative cultural evolution AI`
- `machine discovered strategies human culture`
- `human machine coevolution`
- `AI substitution cultural variance`

**Typical evidence:** cultural-transmission experiments, agent-based models, evolutionary game theory, social-learning studies, machine-culture theory.

**Include when:** the unit of analysis extends from individuals to persistent cultural transmission or cumulative evolution.

**Noise / exclude:** “AI culture” used merely to describe workplace adoption or popular discourse.

### R8. Collective Intelligence & Human–AI Groups
**Core question:** Does adding AI to groups increase collective problem-solving, coordination, diversity, or innovation—and under what information structures?

**Canonical searches:**
- `collective intelligence AI`
- `human AI collective intelligence`
- `human AI groups`
- `AI mediated collective problem solving`
- `collective innovation AI transmission`
- `human AI group decision making`

**Typical evidence:** group experiments, network studies, coordination simulations, collective-discovery tasks, information-routing research.

**Include when:** outcomes are measured at group/population level rather than only at the individual level.

**Noise / exclude:** multi-agent AI benchmarks with no human or collective-cognition relevance.

### R9. Social Learning, Norms & Cultural Feedback
**Core question:** How do AI outputs propagate through people and institutions to change language, norms, beliefs, attention, and future training data?

**Canonical searches:**
- `AI social learning humans`
- `AI norm formation`
- `LLM language change humans`
- `human AI feedback loops`
- `algorithmic mediation culture`
- `AI generated content cultural feedback`

**Typical evidence:** language-corpus studies, norm experiments, recommender-system audits, social-learning research, population-level content studies.

**Include when:** the mechanism contains a feedback path such as `human → AI → human → culture` or `AI → social network → norm`.

**Noise / exclude:** generic discussion of “AI changing society” without an identifiable transmission mechanism.

## Cross-Cutting Context Tags

Expertise, writers, researchers, programmers, students, medicine, education, and other populations are **context tags**, not separate top-level domains. For example:

- an expert–novice study may be tagged `R1 + #experts`;
- a co-writing ownership experiment may be `R3 + R6 + #writers`;
- a clinical decision-support study may be `R2 + R5 + #medicine`.

This keeps the map mechanism-first while preserving applied context.
